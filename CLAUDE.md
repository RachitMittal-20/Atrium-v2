## What this app is
Atrium v2 turns a floor-plan image into an **editable** 3D model that people can walk through, furnish, and export as 3D and 2D files. It is a zero-cost demo: one Next.js app deployed on Vercel's free tier, with no separate backend. OCR runs in the browser (tesseract.js).

## The one architectural rule
Everything is generated from a single `Plan` object (walls, openings, rooms, items, options). The 3D scene, the 2D plan, room areas, schedules, and every export are **derived views** of `Plan`. Never bake geometry into a mesh that can't be regenerated from `Plan`. If a feature seems to need that, stop and ask.

## Conventions
- `Opening.offset` is measured from a wall's `a` end to the opening's centre.
- Walls are split at T-junctions, so every joint is an endpoint of every wall touching it.
- Blueprint pixel rooms are `PixelRoom` (`src/types/blueprint.ts`); plan rooms are `Room` (`src/types/plan.ts`).
- The blueprint pipeline runs on the deskewed image. Coordinates from vectorize, scale and openings are in deskewed-pixel space, so the review screen must display the deskewed image, never the original.
- Pixel to plan: `buildPlan` maps deskewed pixels to metres as x = (px − origin.x) / pxPerM, y = (py − origin.y) / pxPerM, where origin is the top-left of the walls' bounding box. No flip: plan y points south, the same way image y points down.
- OCR reads the deskewed but UNFILLED image; the hollow-wall fill also fills some letter strokes. In hollow mode, windows drawn as lines inside walls are filled as wall, so their openings are not detected.

## Editing (step 4.3, Select)
- Walls are selected in both views but edited only in 2D: clicking a wall mesh in 3D selects it, and **dragging in 3D is not implemented**. The 3D scene tints the selection and nothing more.
- Clicking a piece selects THAT piece and the panel edits it, but the whole run is outlined in a lighter gilt in 2D and tinted lighter in 3D, because a body drag from any piece moves the run. Delete removes only the selected piece. `useSelectedRun()` (`src/store/selectionStore.ts`) is the one place that derives it.
- Selection lives in `src/store/selectionStore.ts`, not in the plan, so it is never undoable. It clears itself when the selected wall leaves the plan.
- The drag maths is pure, in `src/lib/plan/edit.ts`; components only turn pointers into metres and call the store. A drag is one undo step because every pointer move re-applies the whole drag after `planStore.rollback()`.

## Drawing walls (step 4.4, Wall tool)
- The active tool lives in `src/store/toolStore.ts` (`select` | `wall`), not in the plan, so switching is never undoable. Choosing Wall clears the selection; choosing it while only 3D shows opens Split (2D on a phone), because drawing happens only in the 2D plan.
- Click (or tap) to start, click again to add a wall; each click after that adds a joined wall. The chain ends on Escape, double-click, the Finish button (for touch), or by itself when a click lands on a wall already in the plan (closing a room). A second Escape with nothing being drawn goes back to Select. A drag still pans. Implemented but not covered by any test: Enter with nothing typed also ends the chain, and lifting fingers after a pinch is not read as a tap.
- With a wall started, typing a length (3.5, 350 cm, 11' 6", read by `parseTypedLength`) and pressing Enter adds a wall exactly that long towards the pointer. Needs a mouse: on touch there is no pointer direction.
- Where a click lands is `snapDraw` (`src/lib/plan/edit.ts`): `snapPoint`'s order (endpoint, midpoint, 45°/90° ray, 5 cm grid) with one step after midpoint, **on wall**. A pointer within snap reach of a wall's body lands exactly on its centre line; if a 45°/90° ray also crosses that wall in reach, the crossing is used. Landing within 0.2 m of a wall's end takes the end itself. Joint and wall landings keep exact coordinates so they stay shared; free landings are on the grid or a ray; Alt turns snapping off and rounds to 1 cm.
- `planStore.drawWall(a, b, size)` is the only way a drawn wall enters the plan: one transaction, so one undo step. An end that lands on a wall's middle (`hostAt`) splits that wall there first (`splitWall` / `splitWallAt`): the first piece keeps the old id, the second is new, and openings past the split move to it at the same place. The result is an ordinary `Wall`; rooms re-derive through the normal wall-edit path.
- `drawProblem` refuses, with a message, a wall under 0.2 m, a wall that crosses or runs along an existing wall, and a T-junction inside a door or window or within 0.2 m of a wall's end. A refused or cancelled wall records nothing; the preview (outline, snap marker, guide line, live length) is local to PlanCanvas.
- New walls are 0.15 m thick (`DRAW_THICKNESS`) and take the plan's commonest wall height (2.7 m in an empty plan). Change either in the panel afterwards.
- When a chain ends, the panel lists anything it left unjoined (a free end is a validator problem), the same as after a drag.

## Doors and windows (step 4.5)
- **Swing side is data.** `Opening.swing` is `"left" | "right"`, the side of the wall a door's leaf opens to, looking from the wall's `a` end to its `b` end. Which JAMB it pivots on is a separate field, `Opening.hinge` (step M.1d, below); before that it was always the gap end nearer `a`, which is still what an absent `hinge` means. Doors must have a swing and windows must not; `validatePlan` reports either mistake, and ignores `hinge`. `doorSwing` (`src/lib/plan2d/openings.ts`) and the 3D leaf in `PlanModel` both read both, through the one `doorSides`.
- **Migration rule:** a door with no valid side gets `LEGACY_SWING = "left"` (`src/data/samplePlan.ts`), the side the 2D plan always drew. `withSwingSides` (`src/lib/plan/edit.ts`) applies it in `planStore.loadPlan` and to the initial plan, `buildPlan` stamps it on imported doors, and the sample's doors carry it explicitly. Existing plans look the same in 2D. In 3D the leaf used to stand open towards the right, disagreeing with 2D; it now follows the stored side, so migrated doors' 3D leaves turn the other way than before this step.
- **Flip** (`planStore.flipDoor`, the panel's Flip swing button or its Left/Right control) reverses only the side: wall, width, offset, height and sill stay. One undo step. It does nothing to a window.
- **Placing:** the Door and Window tools work in the 2D plan only, not in 3D. The pointer picks the nearest wall whose band (half its thickness plus 8 px) holds it. `snapOpening` projects onto that wall's centre line and snaps the centre to the wall's midpoint within snap reach, else to 0.05 m along the wall. The preview shows the opening and its width; a click or tap places a default door (0.9 × 2.1 m) or window (1.2 × 1.2 m on a 0.9 m sill) with `planStore.placeOpening`, one undo step. Escape goes back to Select.
- **Constraints** (`placeOpening`, `openingProblem`): an opening must sit inside its wall's **usable span**. Each end of the wall loses half the thickness of the thickest non-collinear wall joined there (more at a slanted joint, at most 4 half-thicknesses). A collinear neighbour and a free end take nothing. A centre outside the span (on or past a wall end, or in a corner) is refused with a reason; a centre inside it slides just far enough to fit. A wall too short for it, or an overlap with an opening already there, is refused. Widths are 0.3–4 m; a door is at least 1.8 m tall; a window at least 0.3 m; sill + height never goes above the wall.
- **Selecting:** every Select-tool press and hover goes through `pickTarget` (`src/lib/plan/edit.ts`), which picks in this order:
  1. A wall end handle.
  2. A door or window. Its stretch of wall band counts (half the wall's thickness plus the pick tolerance across, only 1 cm past the gap's ends). So does a door's swing quarter-circle, grown by the pick tolerance on every side so the drawn leaf and arc are clickable from either side. The tolerance is 8 px for mouse and 12 px for touch.
  3. A wall body.
  4. Nothing, which clears the selection.

  The swing loses only to a press physically on a wall (within half its thickness), never to one merely within a wall's pick tolerance; at low zoom that tolerance covers the drawn leaf. Picking is geometry on the pointer's plan coordinates, handled on the `<svg>` root, so SVG stacking and `pointer-events` never decide a hit.

  Selection state: `selectionStore.selectedId` is the selected wall and `openingId` the selected opening. Choosing one clears the other, and empty space or Escape clears both. Neither is in the plan or its history. The selected opening is outlined in cyanotype with its symbol redrawn in cyanotype (a door's leaf and arc, a window's three lines); a selected wall is gilt. Its 3D frame is tinted gilt, and `OpeningPanel` replaces the Summary.
- **Editing** (`OpeningPanel`): type, the wall it sits on, width, height, sill (windows only; doors start at the floor), the centre's distance from the wall's start, and for doors the swing side and Flip; Delete removes only the opening and leaves its wall. Typed values go through `resizeOpening` (keeps the centre if it fits, else shifts, else clamps to the free stretch between neighbours and span ends), `slideOpening` and `openingSize`, are rounded to 1 cm, and land with one `updateOpening`, so each is one undo step; a clamp says why. Dragging a selected opening slides it along its wall, in 0.05 m steps (Alt: free), stopping at its neighbours and the span ends, and rounds to 1 cm on release: one undo step. The Delete key deletes the selected opening.
- **Wall edits and splits:** wall edits still clamp openings with `clampOpening`, so a moved or resized wall never leaves an opening outside it, and the swing side is untouched. A 4.4 split (`splitWallAt`) keeps openings before the split on the first piece (the old id) and moves openings past it to the new piece at the same place. An opening is never duplicated, dropped or detached, and a split through one is refused. Rooms come from walls only, so openings never change them.

## Measure and autosave (step 4.6)
- The temporary Measure overlay lives in `src/store/toolStore.ts` beside the active tool (`select` | `wall` | `door` | `window` | `measure`), never in the plan, so they are not undoable and not saved. A measurement is two points in plan metres, so the 2D camera draws it correctly at any zoom. Pure maths: `src/lib/plan2d/measure.ts`. In Measure mode walls are not picked, hovered or dragged, dragging empty space still pans, and Escape (or the Clear button, for touch) cancels. Entering or leaving the tool clears it; choosing Measure from 3D opens the 2D plan like the other tools. In Measure mode the press is handled before `pickTarget`, so no wall or opening is picked, hovered or dragged. Measure points snap to wall ends and midpoints only, never to a grid, so the value is the real distance.
- Autosave stores ONLY the `Plan`, as `{ schema, savedAt, plan }` under one localStorage key (`src/lib/persist/planStorage.ts`). `src/store/persistence.ts` restores it once per page load, before the editor mounts, and writes after an 800 ms quiet time (at most 5 s apart). Anything corrupt, from another schema or the wrong shape is ignored and the sample plan stays. To change the stored shape, bump `SCHEMA_VERSION` and add a case to `migrate`. Restoring goes through `loadPlan`, which keeps stored room names by matching loops and label points; undo history is never saved or restored.

## Walkthrough collision (step 5.1)
- `src/lib/walk/collision.ts` is pure and in plan metres. Like every other view it is derived from `Plan`. `getCollision(plan)` rebuilds only when `plan.walls` or `plan.openings` is a different array (Immer shares unchanged ones). Item edits and renames reuse the last build; any wall or opening edit, or an undo of one, rebuilds it. `collisionStats.builds` counts rebuilds for tests.
- Solids: each wall's centre line `[0, length]` minus every passable **door** span (`offset ± width/2`), as boxes of the wall's thickness. An interval end that is a wall end (not a door cut) extends by half the thickness, so L-corners, T-stems and free ends close with no notch. Window spans stay solid. A door's leaf is never collision.
- `CAMERA_RADIUS = 0.2 m`. That keeps the near plane out of walls and still leaves 0.4–0.5 m of play in a 0.8–0.9 m door. A door passes only if it is at least `2 × radius + 0.1 = 0.5 m` wide (`MIN_DOOR_WIDTH`). Narrower doors stay solid and are listed in `narrowDoors`.
- `moveWithCollision` treats each solid as a rectangle grown by the radius, with **square** corners. It splits the move into substeps of at most `radius / 2`, so it never tunnels. Each substep moves, then pushes out along the shortest exit normal of every grown rectangle it is in, for up to 4 passes. A substep still overlapping by more than 0.1 mm is undone and the move stops, so a free start never ends inside a solid. The camera's centre is also kept inside the **fence**: the solids' bounding box grown by 1 m, so you can step out of a front door but not wander off.
- `clearance` is the true Euclidean distance to the nearest solid (negative inside). `freePointNear` (within 2 m, else null) is what to call when an edit drops a wall on the camera. `startPose` picks the largest room by net area. It starts at the room's labelPoint if that is clear, else at the clearest 0.1 m grid point, and faces the longest clear run of 16 headings. `eyeHeight` = min(1.6, 0.9 × the lowest wall within 3 m).
- Tested by `scripts/test-walk.ts`, which also proves a naive mover (no substeps, summed pushes) fails the slide, split-wall and tunnelling cases.

## Walkthrough controls (step 5.1)
- Camera mode lives in `src/store/viewStore.ts` (`orbit` | `walk`, pose, saved orbit camera, eye height, notice). It is view state: never undoable and **never persisted**. Autosave subscribes to planStore only, so the payload stays `{ schema, savedAt, plan }` (checked in `scripts/test-walk-input.ts`).
- `enterWalk` starts at `startPose` (the largest room). With no closed room it refuses and shows "Walk needs at least one closed room." `WalkControls` (`src/components/three/WalkControls.tsx`) saves the orbit camera (position, rotation, target, fov, near) in a store subscription the moment the mode flips, and puts it back **exactly** on exit. Walking uses fov 70 and near 0.05.
- Movement maths is pure in `src/lib/walk/input.ts` (`walkStep`): 1.4 m/s, 2.8 m/s with Shift, 1.8 rad/s key turn, 0.0025 rad/px look, pitch ±80°, heading in (-π, π], dt capped at 0.05 s, diagonals not faster, a 10% joystick dead zone, and velocity smoothing τ = 0.08 s (none under reduced motion) with a snap to rest below 0.02 m/s. Forward is (cos h, sin h). Strafe right is (−sin h, cos h): plan y points down the screen, so right is clockwise.
- **Collision from the live plan every frame:** each frame reads `usePlanStore.getState().plan` and calls `getCollision` (memoised). When that returns a new build (a wall or opening edit, or an undo of one), the eye height is recomputed. If the camera is now within radius − 1 mm of a solid, it moves to `freePointNear(…, 2)`, else to the start pose, with the notice "A wall moved onto you, so we moved you." (cleared after 4 s).
- **Fixed eye height:** `eyeHeight` at entry, recomputed only when the collision rebuilds, so walking past a low wall never bobs the view.
- **Keys only with canvas focus:** W A S D, left/right arrows, Shift and Escape are read on the focusable host `<div>` Scene3D wraps around the canvas (`data-testid="scene-3d"`, tabIndex 0, focused on click and on entering walk). They are ignored in form fields and with Ctrl/Cmd/Alt held, and released on blur and when the tab is hidden. The 2D canvas keeps its own arrow keys.
- **No pointer lock:** looking is a drag on the canvas with pointer capture, so it works in Split. On touch, a joystick bottom-left (96 px base, writes `walkJoystick`) moves and strafes while a second finger drags anywhere else to look. `touch-action: none` on the canvas while walking.
- **Picking disabled while walking:** no wall or opening is picked, hovered or tinted, and a pointer miss does not clear the selection. OrbitControls is off and ViewCamera (FitCamera before step V.1) never moves the camera.
- The Orbit | Top | Walk toggle (Top since step V.1), hint line, notice and Exit button are in `src/components/studio/WalkOverlay.tsx`, top-left of the 3D pane (Recenter top-right), beside the canvas host and not inside it. They are hidden with the pane in the 2D-only view, and switching to 2D-only ends the walk.
- Entering walk turns ceilings on and restores the previous setting on exit (`src/app/studio/page.tsx`); the View popover can still toggle them. Ceilings cast no shadows. A neutral ambient fill (0.6) is added only while walking, because a downward-facing ceiling got only the hemisphere's beige ground colour and read as dark brown.
- Dev only: `window.__walkDebug` (mode, ghost, pose, camera position / forward / right, clearance at the camera, and `teleport(x, y, heading)` for TEST SETUP). A production build contains no `__walkDebug`.

## Walk through walls (step G.1)
- **Ghost is a view setting, default OFF.** `viewStore.ghost`, set only by `setGhost`, which refuses (returns false) outside walk. `enterWalk` and `exitWalk` both set it to false, so it never outlives a walk and every walk starts stopped by walls. It is never in the plan, its history or the autosave.
- **An option on the call, never a change to the model.** `moveWithCollision(c, from, delta, { ghost: true })` skips the solids and keeps only the fence (clamped once; per axis that equals clamping each substep). `buildCollision`, the memo and `collisionStats.builds` are untouched, and with no option every result is the old one (`test-walk.ts` (a)–(l) run unchanged). Eye height (fixed), speeds, smoothing and the stop-dead rule, the joystick: unchanged. No flying.
- **The rescue is one pure function**, `rescuePoint(plan, c, p)` (`collision.ts`): p itself when its clearance is at least radius − `RESCUE_SLACK` (1 mm), else `freePointNear(c, p, 2)`, else the start pose with its heading, else null (walking stops with "Walk needs at least one closed room."). WalkControls uses it after a rebuild (skipped while ghost is on: walls don't count) and the moment ghost turns OFF during a walk, with the existing notice "A wall moved onto you, so we moved you."; the eye height is not recomputed then. The nearest clear point can be OUTSIDE the building: from inside a corner joint it is 0.3 m out, nearer than the room's side.
- **UI:** "Walk through walls" (`data-testid="walk-ghost"`, aria-pressed, `aria-keyshortcuts="G"`, 44 px) in WalkOverlay's top row, which wraps on a phone; natively disabled outside walk, and below the `sm` breakpoint (640 px) hidden outside walk (`max-sm:hidden`), because the disabled button sat over the 3D view at 390 and swallowed taps on what was behind it (step G.1-fix; it stays in the DOM, so `isDisabled` still holds). From `sm` up it is always shown. While on: "Walls are ignored. Turn this off to be stopped by walls." (`walk-ghost-status`). G (`viewStore.GHOST_KEY`) toggles it only with the 3D host focused, like W A S D; `test-walk-input.ts` checks it is no tool's key, no walking key and no typed-value key.

## 3D Push/Pull (step 4.7a)
- **A tool, not selection.** `pushpull` is in the `toolStore` tool union (never persisted, shortcut P, enabled in 3D and Split, disabled with the tooltip "Push/Pull works in the 3D view" in 2D-only, and the tool steps back to Select if the 3D pane goes away). While it is active `PlanModel` neither selects nor hovers walls, and nothing here touches the selection store. It is inert while walking. `PushPullTool` (inside the canvas) turns pointer events into calls; `src/store/pushPullStore.ts` holds the hover, the pull and the typed text; the rules are pure in `src/lib/plan/pushpull.ts` and the pointer maths in `src/lib/handles3d/math.ts`.
- **Face roles come from the mesh builder.** `buildWallMeshData` (`src/lib/plan/meshBuilders.ts`) gives every triangle exactly one role (`top`, `endA`, `endB`, `sideLeft`, `sideRight`, `jambA`, `jambB`, `head`, `sill`) where it slices the wall around its openings, in `geometry.userData.faces` (so it survives the per-wall geometry cache), and `faceRoleAt` reads it. Left is the plan normal (-dy, dx), as everywhere. Never infer a face from a normal at pick time. A box's hidden inside caps are named by the way they face, so each role means one outward normal.
- **One pull is one transaction.** Every pointer move rolls the previous preview back (only while it is still the newest history entry) and applies the whole pull again from the plan as it was at the start, as a 2D drag does. A commit leaves one undo step; Escape, pointercancel, window blur, a second finger and leaving the tool roll back and leave the history length as it was (as with a 2D drag, starting an edit has already dropped the redo steps).
- **Distances.** The pointer is the pull along the face's normal through the grab point (`rayAxisParam`), measured from the grab so the face never jumps. The method is chosen ONCE at the grab: a ray within 8° of the axis uses the pixel fallback for the whole pull, so it can never switch mid-pull (near 8° the ray method is about 7 times the fallback for a vertical axis, exact = fallback / cos ψ, so switching would jump). Distances snap to 0.05 m; Alt turns snapping off (1 cm) and means "this piece only".
- **Typed distance.** Digits, a point, a sign and unit letters (m, cm, mm, ft, in) fill a field, Enter applies exactly that signed distance, Backspace edits, Escape cancels, and the pointer is ignored while there is text. It is the wall panel's parser (`parseTypedLength`) plus a sign, decimal feet and a lone inch figure (`parseSignedDistance`). **Keys count only when the 3D host has focus and not in a text field.** On a touch screen the label's "123" button opens a real input.
- **The wall top** changes the height of the whole straight wall (`wallRun`), or only the picked piece with Alt: the same distance is added to each piece, each held to `HEIGHT_RANGE` (1 to 6 m) with `clampField`'s reason on the label, and to 1.8 m where the wall has a door. Openings that no longer fit a lowered wall are shortened, or a window's sill slides down, by the rules of `openingSize`, inside the same transaction. This is done by the tool, not by `planStore.updateWall`, which re-clamps openings only when a wall's LENGTH changes.
- **Limits in 4.7a:** only the wall top was wired; 4.7b wired the opening faces and added Move (next section); 4.7c wired wall sides and free ends (below). A second finger cancels a pull, but the two-finger orbit that follows starts only on the next touch. A wall face's highlight is its triangles by role, not an arbitrary coplanar patch. The tool is not tested on a real phone, only with Chromium's touch events.

## Door and window faces, Move, 3D selection (step 4.7b)
- **Sign convention:** a positive distance makes the OPENING bigger in that direction: a side outwards (wider), the top up (taller), the sill DOWN (a lower sill, a taller window). The label always states the resulting value ("Width 1.10 m", "Height 2.10 m", "Sill 0.60 m") and which edge stays ("Fixed: the side nearer the wall's start" or "…end", "Fixed: the sill" or "the floor", "Fixed: the top").
- **The opposite edge stays fixed**, as in SketchUp: a side moves only that side (`edit.resizeOpeningEdge`; the panel's `resizeOpening` keeps the centre instead, which `scripts/test-pushpull-openings.ts` shows), the top moves only the top (`resizeOpeningHead`), the sill only the bottom (`resizeOpeningSill`). Each returns `{ ok, value, reason }`: `ok` is false only for a refusal (a door's sill: "A door stays on the floor"; an opening that is gone); a clamp is ok, with its reason. The limits are the panel's: width 0.3–4 m, the usable span ("Stopped at the end of the wall"), a neighbour ("Next to another opening"), a door at least 1.8 m and a window 0.3 m tall, the sill at least 0, sill + height at most the wall ("Wall is 2.70 m high"); results round to 1 cm. `jambA` is the side nearer the wall's a end: from the default camera the south window's RIGHT jamb is jambA, because w-EL runs east to west.
- **Axes** (`pushpull.faceAxis`): sides, and Move, along the wall's direction through the opening's centre at mid-height; top and sill along world Y. The ray-or-pixels method, 0.05 m snapping (Alt: 1 cm), typed distances, one transaction per pull, and Escape / pointercancel / blur / a second finger all work as for the wall top. A commit on a door or window selects it, so the OpeningPanel shows its exact numbers.
- **Edge bands** (`src/lib/handles3d/edges.ts`), because a jamb is seen almost edge-on: the hole's edges (both sides, the top, and a window's sill; a door has no sill edge) on the wall face looking at the camera, projected to the screen. `resolveOpeningEdge` takes the nearest within 14 px (mouse) or 44 px (coarse pointer); ties go jamb, then head, then sill. An edge hidden by a wall (a raycast to its nearest point, 5 cm slack) is skipped. An edge beats any face of its own straight wall and any face behind it; a face of ANOTHER wall in front of it keeps the pointer, whether under it or found by a finger's touch rings (on a phone a door just over a nearer wall's top is a few px from that top); with no face under the pointer the nearer in px wins, the edge on a tie. Push/Pull also has a band on a door's bottom, which only says "A door stays on the floor". The highlight is the reveal itself (`openingFaceQuad`), light blue, plus the edge as a 3 px line drawn by `PushPullOverlay`. Not the mesh triangles of the role: the sill and lintel slices' hidden caps share the jamb roles and showed through as full-height lines.
- **Move tool** (`move` in the tool union, never persisted, key V): doors and windows only, along their own wall only. `edit.moveOpening` = the 2D tool's snapping (the wall's midpoint within 12 px at the opening's depth, else 0.05 m; Alt: 1 cm) then `slideOpening`'s limits and reasons. It hovers an opening's frame, leaf or glass (an opening within one wall thickness behind the nearest wall wins), its edge bands, or a wall face that belongs to it; over a bare wall it says "Wall moves arrive with the side faces" and does nothing. Label: the distance moved and "Centre 2.65 m from the wall start". A typed distance goes the way the pointer last moved; before any movement + is towards the wall's b end, and the label says which ("Typed: towards the wall's end →"). Shortcut V, not M: m is a unit letter in typed distances. `toolStore.TOOL_SHORTCUTS` is the one list of tool keys; a test checks no two tools share one.
- **Both 3D tools** (`toolStore.is3dTool`) are aria-disabled with a tooltip in the 2D-only view ("Move works in the 3D view") and while walking ("Exit Walk to use Move"); entering walk switches the tool to Select.
- **3D selection** (the Select tool, and the 2D tools): one handler on PlanModel's group decides with `edges.pickAlongRay`: the nearest wall or opening, except that an opening up to one wall thickness behind the nearest wall wins. Every door and window mesh (frame, leaf, glass) carries `userData.openingId`; walls keep `wallId`. Only a real click (`math.isClick`: under 5 px and 500 ms) selects, so an orbit drag never does (before 4.7b an orbit drag that ended on a wall selected it). A click on a floor or empty ground clears the selection. The selected opening's frame is gilt, a hovered one lighter, the Move tool's target light blue. Split's 2D pane follows, as the selection store is shared.
- **Dev hooks** (never in production): `window.__pushPullDebug` (now set up in `toolStore.ts`) = `{ tool, hover: { wallId, openingId, role } | null, hoverFace (the 4.7a shape), active }`; `window.__studio3d.openingBox(id)` = the world box of an opening's meshes.
- **Limits in 4.7b:** (wall sides, free ends, wall moves and corners arrived in 4.7c, below.) A door's bottom is fixed. There is no vertical move of an opening other than its sill face (the top and sill change its size; Move only slides it along its wall). Openings are still placed only in 2D. Hiding is checked at one point of an edge, the one nearest the pointer. Not tested on a real phone.

## Wall faces, wall moves and corners (step 4.7c)
- **Hidden faces are never targets.** `buildWallMeshData` marks every triangle `exposed` or not (`WallFaceData.exposed`), and the tools use `pickableFaceAt`, which is null for a hidden triangle; a raycast goes on to the next hit, and the highlight skips them. Not exposed: the sill and lintel slices' own end caps; the bands of the solid slice's cap beside an opening that those slices cover (the cap is cut at the sill and the head, so its exposed band is exactly the jamb); and a wall end (`endA`/`endB`) at a joint another wall also ends at (a split, a T stem, a corner, a crossing: `geometry.isFreeEnd`). Only free ends are exposed. The rule reads only the walls at a wall's two ends, which `wallSignature` already includes, so the geometry cache stays valid. `faceRoleAt` still gives every triangle's role. The opening highlight is still the 4.7b reveal quad.
- **Side-face sign convention:** a positive distance pulls a side face OUTWARD, away from the wall's centre line.
- **Thickness keeps the opposite face fixed, for the whole run** (`pushpull.pullWallSide`): every piece of the straight run (`wallRun`) gets its own thickness plus d, and `dragWallBody` slides the run's centre line d/2 towards the pulled side, so walls joined at its ends or standing on it stretch by the existing rules. `planStore.updateWall` with only a thickness would move BOTH faces by d/2 (the failing-first test in `scripts/test-pushpull-walls.ts`). d is held to every piece's 0.05–0.6 m (the intersection; on a tie the picked piece's reason is shown) and to the 0.2 m minimum length of any stretched wall (`dragWallBody`'s limit, then cut back to whole centimetres). Alt only turns snapping off on a side: a piece-only thickness would step its centre off the run's straight line.
- **Shift on a side, and the Move tool on a side, move the run sideways** by d with its thickness unchanged (`dragWallBody` by d). The label says "Thickness 0.35 m (opposite face fixed)" or "Wall moves 0.50 m", the run's piece count, which rooms change ("Bedroom 1 shrinks to 14.4 m²", two named and the rest counted) and any clamp reason. Shift is tracked like Alt and latched while a distance is typed (an inch mark needs Shift). A typed distance on a wall side goes along that side's outward normal.
- **End faces only for free ends** (`pushpull.pullWallEnd`): the end moves along the wall's own line, the other end fixed, never under 0.2 m ("Stopped here: …"). The label gives the run's length. Pulling an a end shifts the wall's openings' offsets by the same amount so they stay where they were. An end at a joint is hidden in 3D and refused by the store (`HIDDEN_END`).
- **Corners are dragged with the Move tool on the ground plane.** Every distinct joint (`pushpull.planCorners`) is a vertical line from the floor to its tallest wall's top; `edges.resolveCorner` picks the nearest within 14 px (44 px for a finger) unless a wall more than its thickness + 5 cm in front hides it. The drag is on the horizontal plane through the grab point (`math.rayPlanePoint`; when the ray misses the plane the last valid position is held), measured from the grab, then `pushpull.dragCorner` = `edit.snapDrag` (endpoint, midpoint, 45°/90° from the grabbed wall's other end, 5 cm grid; Alt: 1 cm) + `edit.dragEndpoint`, exactly the 2D handle drag: every wall joined there follows and walls at other angles tilt. The line is drawn 3 px blue with a marker at its foot; the label gives the snap and up to three joined walls' lengths. No typed distance: the status line says "Type exact lengths in the wall panel". One transaction, Escape rolls back.
- **Move tool order:** an opening's own meshes, then an opening edge, then a corner, then a wall face; over a wall top Move says "Pull the top with Push/Pull", over a free end "Drag the corner to move a wall end". The order holds within the precise 14 px; a finger's extra reach (14–44 px) goes to whichever is nearer, and a finger right on a wall face keeps it unless a corner is within 14 px. Without this, at phone scale the 44 px bands covered most of the model. A finger's rings of rays (no face right under it) prefer a face that can be pulled and then the one nearest the camera: since every wall side can be pulled, ray order alone could pick a wall behind the one the finger is next to (it broke the 4.7a 390 test, which aims 10 px past a wall top).
- **Dev hook:** `window.__pushPullDebug` also has `corner` (the hovered or dragged corner's plan point, or null) and `exposed` (whether the hovered face can be seen, or null).
- **Limits in 4.7c:** no vertex or face editing of imported meshes (there are none; everything is the Plan). Wall ends at joints are not directly pullable: drag the corner instead. Walls that overlap without sharing a joint (drawn parallel and too close) keep their buried side faces exposed. A wall end at a joint is hidden even where it stands taller than the wall it meets. A corner's hiding is checked at one point of its line. Not tested on a real phone.

## Imported 3D models (step I.1a)
- **Imported models are meshes only.** An imported model is an `Item` with `import` set (`ImportInfo` in `src/types/plan.ts`) and `catalogId = "import:" + assetId`. Walls, rooms, `validatePlan`, collision (walk mode passes through them) and schedules ignore them. `Item.position` is world space as the type says: x = plan x, **y = height**, z = plan y. It is where the model's **base point** goes: the bottom centre of the WHOLE model's bounding box after unit and up axis (`model.modelFrame`), so deleting a part never moves the rest. `rotationY` turns and `scale` (uniform) grows it about that point. Store actions: `addImportedItem`, `setNodeOverride` (plus `updateItem`, `deleteItem`), one undo step each; "Restore N deleted objects" is one `transaction`.
- **The bytes live in IndexedDB, never in the autosave.** `src/lib/import/assetStore.ts`: database `atrium-assets`, keyed by assetId = the first 16 hex characters of the SHA-256 of the bytes (several files: each name and its bytes, in name order), so the same file is stored once. It stores the files exactly as picked (a model, a model plus its .mtl and textures, or one .zip) and loads them back through the same `loadModel`. The autosave stays `{ schema, savedAt, plan }` with only the settings in `Item.import` (`planStorage.item` checks them strictly). Storing happens BEFORE the item is added: a browser that can't store (blocked, private, full) shows a plain message and adds nothing. Assets are kept when an item is deleted so Undo works. Files never leave the browser.
- **One parsed scene per asset per session** (`src/lib/import/assetCache.ts`), shared by the 3D view, the 2D footprints and the panel: loading, ready, missing (no bytes: a grey 1 m box named "Missing file: <name>", and the panel says "Import it again to see it") or error. Each item in 3D draws a SkeletonUtils clone with its overrides applied and a material copy per mesh (`ImportedItems.tsx`); the cached original is never drawn or changed. Every clone node carries its ORIGINAL path in `userData.importPath`, because deleting nodes shifts the clone's indices. Disposal: an item's material copies when it goes or is rebuilt; the shared geometries and textures when the last item using the asset goes (`acquire` / `release`, deferred one task). `<primitive dispose={null}>` everywhere. Shadows only under 200k triangles. Hidden parts get a no-op `raycast` (three's raycaster ignores `visible`).
- **Node paths and nodeOverrides.** A node's path is its child indices from the model root joined by "/" ("0/3/1"; `model.nodePaths`), the same on every parse of the same file. `nodeOverrides[path] = { hidden?, deleted? }`; `applyOverrides` resolves all paths before removing anything, and only ever runs on a CLONE. Unnamed nodes are listed as "Object N" (N = place among siblings; GLTFLoader already names unnamed meshes `mesh_N`).
- **The dialog** (`ImportModelDialog.tsx`, a native `<dialog>` with `showModal()`, so the page is inert and Escape closes it): the name, an orbitable preview (scaled to 2 m), Unit (m, cm, mm, in, ft, plus the file's own when it states one that is none of these) with each option's size "w × d × h m" and the chosen one under the list, Up axis (Y / Z, from the file; STL and 3DS start at Z, everything else that says nothing at Y), "Show both sides of faces" (off), "Place on the floor" (on: height 0; off: the model keeps its own height), the warnings and the stats. **Unit guess** (`model.guessUnit`, rule from step I.1b): see "Imported models: move, rotate, scale, parts" below; every candidate is shown with its size so the user picks. The item goes to the centre of the walls' bounding box (the origin with no walls), one undo step, and is selected.
- **Formats and limits** (`src/lib/import/loadModel.ts`, each loader dynamically imported into its own chunk): GLB, glTF (+ .bin), OBJ with MTL, FBX, DAE, STL (ASCII and binary), 3DS, or one .zip of them; textures png, jpg, webp. The extension AND the bytes must agree (`sniffFormat`). Exactly one model per import. One file at most 50 MB; a zip at most 200 MB unpacked (declared sizes; fflate inflates into buffers of the declared size), entries with ".." or an absolute path skipped with a warning, `__MACOSX` and hidden files ignored. Over 500k triangles warns ("may be slow on this device"), over 3M refuses. Draco and Meshopt glTFs are refused ("Export without compression."). A 3DS file with a chunk shorter than its header is refused before three's TDSLoader sees it (it would loop forever on it). Cameras, lights and helpers are removed. COLLADA's own unit and up axis are read from the text and the loader's own root scale and tilt reset, so the dialog applies them. Errors are `ImportError` with plain words.
- **Nothing in a file is fetched or run.** Every URL a loader asks for goes through a LoadingManager URL modifier (`companionManager`): a companion found by path relative to the model, then by file name ignoring case, becomes a blob: URL of the user's own file; anything else (a missing texture, a web address) becomes a 1 px grey PNG and is listed as "Missing file: …". Only data: and blob: URLs pass through. Textures must travel with the model (picked together, or in the zip).
- **.skp and .max are not read yet** (they need a server, later). Choosing one says: ".skp and .max files can't be opened directly yet. In SketchUp use File > Export > 3D Model and choose DAE, OBJ or FBX. In 3ds Max use Export and choose FBX or OBJ. Keep your objects separate and named before exporting." Any other unknown type: "This file type isn't supported."
- **UI:** TopBar Import menu ("Floor plan image…" → /studio/import; "3D model…" → the hidden real `<input type="file" multiple>`, `data-testid="import-model-input"`), or drop files on the 3D pane. A thin bar on top of the 3D pane while a stored model loads. Select tool: a real click selects a model (`selectionStore.itemId`; choosing a wall or opening clears it, and vice versa), gilt tint, lighter on hover; the 3D tools and walking pick nothing there, and Push/Pull hovers nothing over a model nearest the pointer and says "Imported objects are edited with Move, Rotate and Scale" (a press there orbits); Move, Rotate and Scale act on models since I.1b. 2D: each model's footprint (its visible parts' box, turned and scaled, `model.footprint`) is a dashed outline with its name; a click inside one that picks no wall, door or window selects it. `ItemPanel.tsx`: name, format, triangles, size, position x / y / height (the length parser, sign allowed), rotation in degrees, scale 1%–10,000%, Delete model, and the object list (collapsible, mesh counts, eye and delete per row, Restore; clicking a name tints that node light blue for 2.5 s via `importStore.highlight`, never stored).
- **Dev hook** (never in production): `window.__importDebug` = `{ items(), worldBox(id), meshNames(id), missing(id), textureWidths(id), renderer(), assets() }`, set up in `ImportedItems.tsx`.
- **Limits in I.1a:** no automatic cleanup of unused assets in IndexedDB; Draco and Meshopt compression refused; textures must travel with the file or zip; no materials list (piece C); models are not collision in Walk; FBX and 3DS success paths untested until real files are provided; the object list shows at most 400 rows; a material copy per mesh (a model with thousands of meshes has thousands of materials).

## Imported models: move, rotate, scale, parts (step I.1b)
- **Unit rule** (`model.guessUnit`, I.1b-fix): (a) a unit the file declares wins (COLLADA `<unit meter>`, glTF is metres); (b) otherwise the candidates are those of m, cm, mm, in, ft whose largest side is 0.2–60 m; (c) the unit chosen on the LAST import, remembered in localStorage under "atrium.lastImportUnit" (`importStore.readLastUnit`; never in the plan or the autosave; a file's own odd unit is not remembered), wins when it is a candidate; (d) otherwise the metric candidate (m, cm, mm) nearest 1.5 m on a log scale (smallest |ln(size / 1.5 m)|), ties m, mm, cm; (e) only when no metric unit fits, the imperial one nearest 1.5 m (with these five units that never happens: m, cm and mm together fit 0.2–60,000 units, and inches and feet lie inside that); metres when none fits. `ambiguous` when two or more candidates fit and their sizes differ by more than 2× (never with a declared unit). 120 units → cm and ambiguous (inches also fit), 3200 → mm, 320 → cm, 126 → cm and ambiguous, 3.2 → m, 3000 → mm and ambiguous; with the last unit inches, 126 → in. The dialog draws a 1.8 m figure (capsule + sphere) beside the model at the chosen unit's scale, says "Your plan is 10.0 × 8.0 m", and when ambiguous "Several sizes are possible. Compare with the 1.8 m figure." with the guess still chosen.
- **Pure rules** (`src/lib/import/transform.ts`, `{ ok, value, reason }`, metres, plan space): `moveItemFloor` (start + pointer movement, 5 cm grid, 1 cm with Alt), `liftItem` (5 cm, held to 0–10 m, "Can't go below the floor"), `rotateItem` (the CHANGE snapped to 15°, 0.1° with Alt, wrapped to (−π, π]; `pointerAngle` = atan2(−dy, dx) so the sign is the panel's rotation field's and three's rotateY), `scaleItem` (ratio of screen distances from the base point's projection, 1% steps, 0.1% with Alt, 1–10,000%, refused under a 24 px grab: "Grab farther from the base point"). A clamp is ok with its reason; only a refusal is not ok.
- **Tools.** Move (V) also moves imported models; Rotate (Q) and Scale (Z) are models only. `toolStore.ITEM_TOOLS`; `TOOLS_3D` has all four 3D tools; `isPushPullTool` is Push/Pull and Move. Choosing Move, Rotate or Scale keeps a selected model selected (other tools still clear the selection). All are disabled in 2D-only and while walking ("Exit Walk to use Rotate"); entering walk switches to Select.
  - Move's picking stays in `PushPullTool`: it computes its own pick (opening mesh, edge band, corner, wall face, with the existing priorities) and asks `ItemTool`'s `probe` (through `itemToolStore.bridge`) how far the model under the pointer is; the nearer wins (a corner's depth is its grabbed point). A model that wins is hovered and dragged by `ItemTool`.
  - A drag is on the horizontal plane through the base point (`rayPlanePoint`, last valid point held), or with Shift / the Floor | Lift toggle (chosen at the press) along world Y (`startPull` / `pullDistance`). Rotate: the pointer's angle on that plane round the base point. Scale: the screen distance from the base point's projection. A click without a drag selects the model (or part). Coarse pointers: rings at 11 and 22 px (a 44 px target).
  - `src/store/itemToolStore.ts` holds hover, drag, typed text, Alt, the Lift toggle. One drag is one transaction (rollback + re-apply, as Push/Pull); Escape, pointercancel, blur and a second finger roll back with history unchanged. Typed: Rotate turns BY the degrees, Scale sets the percentage ("150" or "150%"), Move takes none ("Type exact positions in the panel"); with no drag they act on the selection (its active part), else the hovered model. Keys only with the 3D host focused; `ItemToolOverlay` has a real input for touch.
  - Labels: "+0.35 m, -0.10 m" / "At x 2.35 m, y 2.90 m"; "+0.50 m" / "Height 0.50 m"; "+15°" / "Rotation 45°"; "Scale 150%" / "Size 1.8 × 1.2 × 1.13 m".
- **Part transforms.** `NodeOverride.transform = { t: [x, y, z], rotY, s }` in the MODEL frame (metres, Y up, after unit and up axis, before the item's position, rotation and scale, so the item carries its parts). Pivot: the centre of the part's ORIGINAL bounding box at its lowest point (`model.partBox` / `pivotOf`, from the cached original, so deleting children never moves it). `X = T(pivot + t) · R_y(rotY) · S(s) · T(−pivot)`. `applyPartTransforms` sets the node's local matrix to `P⁻¹ · X · P · L` (P = the parent's ORIGINAL model-frame matrix, L = the node's own original matrix), set directly (`matrixAutoUpdate = false`, never decomposed, so a sheared parent works); children follow, a part in a moved parent is carried. It undoes earlier transforms first (`userData.importBase`), so it re-runs on every drag without rebuilding the clone; `ImportedItems` rebuilds the clone only when hidden/deleted change. Hidden and deleted rules are unchanged; a deleted parent removes its children but their transforms stay in the plan, so Restore brings them back exactly. A drag (I.1b-fix) is right under any parent chain: the part's pivot is where it is now (`model.partPivotNow`: its own transform, then its ancestors' `model.ancestorTransforms` A), the world change (a move, or a turn or scale about that pivot) is taken into the model frame through the item's turn and scale (`worldToModelDelta`), and `model.partTransformAfter` gives the new own transform X' = A⁻¹ · D · A · X, read back as { t, rotY, s }. `planStore.setNodeTransform` (one undo step; null or the identity removes it; the store rounds offsets to 1 nm); `setNodeOverride` always keeps the transform. Parts move only in 3D and the panel; nothing in 2D changes (the footprint is still the unmoved parts' box).
- **Edit parts and pickPart.** `selectionStore.editParts` / `partPath` (never in the plan). The panel's "Edit parts" toggle (aria-pressed); then a Select click picks a PART with `model.pickPart(root, hit, { deep })`: the highest node below the root with a real name (not "Object N"), skipping groups that only wrap the whole model (the root's only child, and on down); with no named node the mesh's own; Alt-click (`deep`) the mesh's own node. A row's name in the list also picks the part; the active row is marked (`data-active`, gilt) and scrolled into view with its groups opened. Move, Rotate and Scale then act on the part (a press on another part of the same model picks that one). The panel's part rows: x, y, height of the pivot relative to the model's base point (model axes), rotation, scale %, each one undo step, and "Reset part". Escape or the toggle off returns to the whole model; choosing another model leaves Edit parts.
- **Outline and Show in view.** The selected item or part: gilt tint plus a 2 px gilt edge overlay (`LineSegments2` from `EdgesGeometry`, 30°) in two passes (I.1b-fix): the visible edges solid and depth-tested (polygon offset so they win over the faces they lie on), the edges a surface hides at 20% opacity, so back edges never read as solid lines. At the scene root, copying each mesh's world matrix every frame. What a tool would act on is light blue. "Show in view" (and once after every import, only if the model is not fully in view) is `viewStore.requestFrame`; `ImportedItems` moves the orbit camera with `math.frameBox(box, camera, aspect, 1.15)`: from the current direction, raised to at least 5° above horizontal, at least 0.1 m up, gliding 0.45 s (instant under prefers-reduced-motion), never while walking. The framed box stays framed when the pane resizes (a phone's sheet closing) until the user orbits, pans or zooms.
- **Normals** (I.1b-fix): `loadModel.ensureNormals` gives a mesh with no normals, or with any of zero length (an STL that writes 0 0 0 per facet, which lit black), computed ones: flat per face for STL, smooth vertex normals otherwise.
- **Dev hook additions** (never in production): `__importDebug.partWorldBox(id, path)`, `partPivot(id, path)` (a part's world pivot now), `camera()` (position, target, fov, aspect, gliding), `parseMs()`.
- **On-demand tool:** `scripts/import-report.ts` (needs `npm run dev`; reads the git-ignored `test-models/`; not in npm test, not a pre-commit check).
- **Limits in I.1b:** no vertex or face editing of imported meshes; parts cannot be reparented; scaling is uniform only; walk mode ignores imported objects (no collision); parsing runs on the main thread, so a malformed file can still hang a loader (the report script can only notice it); Shift / Lift is chosen at the press, not mid-drag; the panel's typed part x / y / height are the part's own offset in the model frame (an ancestor's part transform is not added to the numbers shown); the outline's edges are rebuilt on selection, slow for multi-million-triangle models; a drag that turns more than 180° wraps; the 2D footprint ignores part transforms; not tested on a real phone, Safari or Firefox.

## Reading PDFs (step M.1a)
- **PDF units are points** (1 pt = 1/72 inch on paper), in the page's viewport at scale 1: the CTM and the page's /Rotate are applied and y points down, like image pixels (`src/types/pdf.ts`). A raster at k px/pt is those coordinates × k. Scale is metres per point.
- **pdf.js runs off the main thread**, in `src/workers/pdf.worker.ts`. That worker imports pdf.js's core (`pdfjs-dist/build/pdf.worker.mjs`) and sets it as `globalThis.pdfjsWorker`, so pdf.js parses in that thread and never sets `workerSrc` or starts a worker of its own. The core also posts a "ready" handshake to the page, which `runPdf` (`importFile.ts`) ignores. Safety options are in `src/lib/pdf/open.ts`: no eval, no font faces, no system fonts, no XFA, no scripting, and no CMap or standard-font URLs. The only fetch is pdf.js's image-decoder WebAssembly from our own `/pdfjs/wasm/` (copied from `pdfjs-dist/wasm`, licences beside it). The page only shows the results; files never leave the browser. Node tests use the legacy build the same way (`scripts/pdf-node.ts`, rendering with `@napi-rs/canvas`).
- **Vector first.** `extract.ts` reads every painted path as straight segments (curves flattened within 0.05 pt) and every text item. It classifies the page: "vector" (meaningful line work: 40+ segments and a total length of at least width + height), "scanned" (the largest image covers 40%+ of the page and the line work is not meaningful), or "mixed" (both, read as vector and said so). **Scanned pages fall back to the image pipeline**: rendered at 300 dpi (at most 40 MP) and run through `analyseBlueprint` unchanged, OCR included, with the note "Scanned PDF: measurements are approximate". Vector pages are rendered at 100 px per metre of the PDF's scale, capped below 2400 px on the long side. `analyseBlueprint` then runs with `straighten: false`, and the PDF's own text (no OCR) is passed in as the room-name labels. The scale is the PDF's, never OCR's.
- **Scale rules** (`src/lib/pdf/scale.ts`). A dimension text (parseLength's forms plus "12 ft 6 in") is matched to the nearest segment in its direction (within 2°), at least 4 text heights long, within 1.5 text heights of the text's centre, and passing beside it. Sample = value / length. The title block gives "1:N" or `1/8" = 1'-0"`, with 1 pt = 25.4/72 mm on paper. Bands are the image ones: good = 2+ samples within 5% of their median; check = one sample or a 5–15% spread; none beyond 15% (step Q.4a: with fewer than 3 samples, good also needs them within 1% of each other). **The title block alone never makes "good"**; with no usable dimensions it gives "check". Good dimensions that differ from the title block by more than 2% are used, with the warning "The title block says 1:100, but the printed dimensions measure 1:115, so the page was probably scaled to fit when printed. Using the dimensions." Bare numbers on dimension lines only feed the "Feet or metres?" question; a PDF also offers millimetres.
- **Dimension check and overrides** (`src/lib/pdf/dimensions.ts`, `DimensionCheck.tsx`). There is one row per matched dimension: printed, measured at the scale in use, the difference in mm and %, and OK within max(2 mm, 0.2%), else Flagged. Units are mm or feet-inches to 1/16", and there is a CSV download. A flagged row can be overridden with a typed value and a note. The override is kept beside the measured value (both in the table and the CSV), the row says "Overridden", and it changes nothing else in M.1a. Overrides live in the import page's state only.
- **Limits.** 50 MB; the page picker lists at most 30 pages; 500,000 path commands per page. Encrypted, damaged, empty and blank pages get plain messages: pdf.js shows a PDF with an empty page tree as one blank page, which is then refused as blank.
- **Limits of M.1a** (as amended by M.1b, below). The image pipeline still traces walls from the raster to about one pixel; since M.1b those traces are only kept where the PDF's own lines don't explain a wall. One page at a time. Text in fonts the PDF does not embed is drawn with the canvas's own font, and pdf.js measures its width with its own metrics.

## Exact walls from vector PDFs (step M.1b)
- **Vector walls come from face pairing** (`src/lib/pdf/walls.ts`, pure, page metres = points × the PDF's metres per point, computed in the PDF worker into `PdfInfo.vectorWalls`; none without a scale). Faces: straight axis-aligned segments (within 0.5°) at least 0.3 m long, stroked or filled edges, collinear pieces joined (1 mm); not faces: curve pieces and door leaves (a segment up to 1.5 m with an end on a curve's end; since M.1c also every arc's pieces and leaf, polyline arcs included). A filled path that is exactly an axis-aligned rectangle 0.05–0.60 m across is a pair as it stands. Otherwise two parallel faces 0.05–0.60 m apart (inclusive) overlapping by at least 60% of the shorter make a piece (centre midway, thickness the gap, span the overlap); pieces under 0.4 m dropped; a piece inside a wider one's band dropped (the widest wins: a window's glass line, hatching). Pieces on one line (centre and thickness within 1 mm) merge across gaps under 0.45 m (0.3 m before M.1c); gaps of 0.45–4 m with nothing crossing become an opening, classified by M.1c; a gap OUTSIDE that range is unchanged but reported (step M.1d, below); a line inside a wall's band away from both faces, 0.45–4 m long, is a window's glass. Clutter: no other piece within 0.3 m of either end AND under 1.5 m long is dropped (and returned, `clutter`). Joints: an end within 0.15 m of a non-parallel wall's footprint moves to the centre-line crossing; then T-junctions split the host and crossings split both. Diagonal and curved walls are NOT read, only counted (`skipped`).
- **Provenance:** `Wall.provenance?: "vector" | "traced"` (absent on hand-drawn, sample and image-imported walls; old plans load unchanged). Editing keeps it; `planStore`'s `edit()` funnel sets `edited: true` the first time a provenance wall's a, b, thickness or height changes (undo removes it with the edit). The wall panel says "Read from PDF", "Traced, approximate" or "Edited" (`data-testid="wall-provenance"`). `planStorage` accepts both fields and drops unknown values.
- **Merge and the 3-pixel snap limit** (`src/lib/pdf/snap.ts`, `buildPdfPlan` on the import page for every vector page with `vectorWalls`; `buildFromAnalysis` otherwise). Traced and vector walls are related when parallel (2°), overlapping by more than 3 px along, and within half the thicker thickness + 3 px across; related walls form groups. A group takes its vector walls only if every traced wall in it has a vector wall within 2 px AND the vector walls reach its ends within 3 px; otherwise the group keeps its traced walls (reason in `coverage.tracedWalls`). Unrelated vector walls are added (missed by the tracing); unrelated traced walls are kept as traced. A traced piece that can't snap is left out (with a note, never sending its group back) when it is a FRAGMENT of the vector wall it lies along (under 80% of its length) or a FACE LINE (under half its thickness), or when it lies wholly inside a vector wall's footprint, on a pair walls.ts rejected as clutter, fails the same clutter rule (under 1.5 m, nothing within 0.3 m), or, when the PDF gave walls, is not joined to any of them through ends or T-junctions. A traced opening goes with its left-out wall, and an opening added from the PDF never overlaps a snapped or traced one. Chromium's raster of plan-clutter traces mostly noise; `tests/fixtures/pdf/clutter-chromium-trace.json` keeps that trace as a regression. Traced ends join vector ends or lines only within 3 px. Openings: a traced one whose both jambs are within 3 px of a vector opening's takes its exact jambs and keeps its own kind; one overlapping a vector opening farther than that stays traced and is reported; vector-only openings are added. **Nothing ever moves more than 3 raster pixels.** Fallback: a vector wall named in a validator problem the traced plan didn't have goes back to traced (or, if added, is left out), up to three rounds; problems are compared without numbers or ".sN" split suffixes, since the origin moves. If problems remain, or fewer rooms derive than from the tracing, the whole traced plan is used, with the reason. Coordinates are rounded to 0.1 mm and vector thicknesses to 0.01 mm (an imperial 0.21336 m stays exact); traced walls keep buildPlan's 1 cm thickness. The plan's origin is the final walls' top-left.
- **Coverage report** (`CoveragePanel` in `PdfPanels.tsx`, shown on the review screen once a scale is in use): "N walls match lines in the PDF (X% of wall length); K walls are traced approximately (about Y mm)" (step H.1 reworded it from "N of M walls read exactly"; `data-walls` still holds M), where Y mm is one raster pixel. It lists the traced walls with their reasons, and the overlay draws them in gilt dashes (PlanViewer's "Traced walls" layer). It also counts diagonal and curved walls skipped, openings snapped, traced and added, and walls added from the PDF, plus the fallback notes.
- **Ticks versus line length** (`scale.ts` `dimensionLine`; since step Q.4a filled arrowheads' tips come first, below): a dimension line broken around its own text (two collinear parts with the text between them, each ending within 2 text heights of it) is read as one line. Its length is measured between ticks or extension lines when both ends have one (segments perpendicular to the line, at least 1 text height long, crossing it within 0.5 text heights of the end), else it is the line's own length. The table says which ("between ticks" or "line length"). A line overshooting its ticks is not over-measured.
- **OCR fallback labelling:** a vector page with meaningful line work but fewer than 3 text items has outlined text. It is rendered at 300 dpi and read by the existing `ocrPlan`. The words become page text (`ocrTexts`: box height over 0.72 em, or over 0.52 em for a word of only short letters such as "mm", so "4000" and "mm" join), and go through the same scale rules. Every such row says "read by OCR", the PDF panel says the text was read by OCR, and the title block alone is still only "check". `textLines` sorts words in reading order, so bottom-to-top words join too. The OCR fetches tesseract's language data from its CDN, as the scanned route does; the PDF never leaves the browser.
- **Model versus printed** (`dimensions.ts` `modelRows`, the second table): each printed dimension whose measured ends land, within 3 raster px along it, on the BUILT model. A landing is a wall running across it (at its centre line or a face) or a door or window jamb. The row compares the model's length between the landings with the printed value: "centre to centre", "inside faces", "outside faces", "opening width" or a mix. Status is the same OK rule; rows on traced walls say "approximate". Overrides work as in the first table (kept by dimension index), units switch, and one CSV holds both tables (the model section after a blank line).
- **Layout:** the review screen is a grid; at 390 the order is plan, then the side panels (warnings, PDF, coverage, scale, Build), then the tables. From lg the tables sit under the plan.
- **Limits:** (step M.2b drops a narrow traced opening where the PDF's wall runs unbroken, so a phantom gap no longer reaches the plan.) Axis-aligned walls only; diagonal and curved walls are skipped (counted, and kept as traced if the image pipeline traced them). (Step Q.2: a FILLED rectangle at any angle is read as a wall.) Hatch-filled walls may need manual review: hatching inside a wall is dropped, but a hatch that confuses the tracing leaves traced extras. Parallel lines closer than 0.6 m beside a wall (a wardrobe front, a duct) can pair into an extra or thicker wall. (Step Q.1: rules 3, 9, 6 and 7 now catch most of these. Furniture backed onto a wall's face is dropped (rule 9); furniture standing END-ON against a wall, whose two long edges pair and whose pair's end joins the wall, still reads as a stub wall with a free end: a known failure, asserted in `test-pdf-conventions.ts`.) A collinear gap of 0.45–4 m with nothing crossing it becomes an opening (M.1c classifies it). A door right at a corner (no wall piece beyond its far jamb) is not bridged. Door swing sides come from the drawn arc since M.1c (below). A traced opening the tracing made too wide is replaced by the PDF's since M.1c (it stayed traced in M.1b). Without a PDF scale, walls are traced only. Tested on synthetic fixtures only.

## Openings from vector PDFs (step M.1c)
- **What the worker keeps.** Only flattened segments: a Bézier swing arrives as short pieces with `curve: true` (and its `path` number), a CAD polyline arc as plain straight pieces. No control points.
- **Arcs** (`src/lib/pdf/openings.ts`, pure, page metres). A chain is segments consecutive in painting order, each starting where the last ended (0.1 mm), each a curve piece or a straight piece of at most 0.6 m, turning at most 45° per joint (either way, so a noisy arc stays one chain), 3+ pieces. Kasa's algebraic circle fit; residual = RMS distance from the circle. It is an **arc** when the residual is under 0.5% of r, r ≥ 0.2 m and the sweep ≥ 30° (smaller curves are lettering, never counted). A **swing arc** also has a sweep of 80–100°, r 0.5–1.5 m, and a **leaf**: a straight segment from the centre to one end of the arc, as long as r within 2% (both ends within 2% of r). Arc pieces and leaves are never wall faces in `walls.ts` (so a polyline door's leaf can't pair as a wall). Every arc no opening uses is reported with why (radius, sweep, no leaf, centre not at a jamb, radius doesn't match): "arcs not used".
- **Openings** are 0.45–4 m (`OPENING_WIDTH`; `walls.ts` now merges pieces closer than 0.45 m as solid wall, where M.1b used 0.3). Spans come from `walls.ts`: a break where both faces end and the walls on both sides carry on along the same centre line (a thinner pair inside such a gap, two glazing lines, neither blocks the bridge nor stays a wall), or a glass line inside an unbroken wall. `classifyOpening`, in order: **door** = an unused swing arc whose centre is within 0.1 m of one jamb (the wall's cross-line there, face to face) and whose radius is the width within 10%; **double** door (one door) = one such arc at each jamb, each half the width within 10%; **window** = 2 or 3 distinct parallel lines inside the wall's thickness each covering ≥ 90% of the gap; **sliding** door = no such lines but 2+ staggered panel lines (each 30–80% of the gap, together jamb to jamb); otherwise **unclassified**: kept as a door with swing "left" (LEGACY_SWING) and flagged "Opening type not drawn; assumed door".
- **Swing derivation, in the existing convention.** "left" = the (-dy, dx) side of the wall's a→b, which on screen (y down) is the visual RIGHT of a→b. The side is the sign of the arc's middle point's offset from the centre line along that normal; `VectorOpening.swingNormal` keeps it as a unit vector and `snap.ts` re-derives `swing` against the final wall's `wallNormal`. `hingeEnd` says which jamb the arc is centred on, and since step M.1d `snap.ts` stores it as `Opening.hinge`: **no wall is ever turned round** to suit a door, so one wall can carry doors hung both ways and every one of them is exact. (M.1c reversed such a wall — a and b swapped, offsets L − offset, door sides flipped with the normal — and mirrored any door still left on a b jamb, with a note.) `doorSwing` and the 3D leaf read `hinge`. The test `test-pdf-openings.ts` pins this on a hand-checked door (leaf north on a west→east wall = "right").
- **Merge** (`snap.ts`, vector primary): a traced opening with both jambs within 3 px of a PDF opening is "snapped"; one overlapping a PDF opening (or within 3 px of it along the line) with a jamb farther out is "replaced" and noted with the distance (plan-lines' 1.54 m door); one with no PDF opening within 3 px stays traced and is listed (`tracedOpenings`); PDF openings the tracing missed are "added". Every PDF opening keeps the PDF's kind, class and swing. A PDF opening `openingProblem` refuses (outside the usable span, overlapping, too narrow) is left out with the reason and its traced counterpart comes back; a traced opening overlapping a PDF one is left out. validatePlan and the rooms are checked as before. A reading from before M.1c (no `class`, e.g. `clutter-chromium-trace.json`) treats a door as unclassified.
- **Opening provenance:** `Opening.provenance?: "vector" | "traced"`, `edited?: true` and `defaultHeight?: true`, as walls' (absent on hand-placed, sample and image-imported openings; old plans load unchanged; `planStorage` drops unknown values). The store's `markEdited` sets `edited` the first time the wall, kind, offset, width, height, sill or swing changes, and clears `defaultHeight` once height or sill changes; undo removes both with the edit. `OpeningPanel` says "Read from PDF", "Traced, approximate" or "Edited", and "Default height" (`data-testid="opening-provenance"`).
- **Default heights:** a PDF has no heights, so every PDF opening (vector or traced) gets the image importer's `DOOR_SIZE` (2.1 m) and `WINDOW_SIZE` (1.2 m on a 0.9 m sill) and `defaultHeight`; the coverage report says so.
- **Review screen:** `CoveragePanel` adds "A openings match features in the PDF (doors D, windows W, unclassified U); C traced approximately; K arcs not used" (step S.1 reworded it from "A of B openings read exactly"; `coverage-openings-line` keeps B in `data-total`, `coverage-openings-text` is the sentence; D counts doors, double and sliding doors), the default-height line, and lists of unclassified openings, traced openings and unused arcs. `PlanViewer` boxes traced openings in gilt and unclassified ones in orange (dashed). The model-versus-printed table's "opening width" rows land on both jambs of one opening; a row on a traced opening's jamb is "approximate".
- **Limits:** axis-aligned walls only (an opening in a diagonal or curved wall is not read). Curved openings, arched doors, bi-fold, pocket and revolving doors are not read (a bi-fold's small arcs fail the radius rule; the gap is unclassified). Door and window heights and sills come from the defaults. Since M.1d a door can hang on either jamb, so nothing is mirrored any more. An opening read from the PDF on a wall that stays traced has the PDF's exact width but the traced wall's position. A door at a corner whose gap is not bridged (M.1b limit) is not read; its arc is "not used". In a whole-plan fallback to traced, arcs that were used are not counted. Tested on synthetic fixtures only (plan-doors.pdf: polyline swings; the others: Bézier swings).

## Quantity takeoffs (step M.2a)
- **Takeoffs are annotations in the plan, never geometry.** `plan.takeoffs?: Takeoff[]` (`src/types/plan.ts`): `{ id, name, kind: "length" | "area", points: [x, y][], cutouts?, reference?, override?, wallsHash }`. Walls, rooms, `validatePlan`, collision and walk ignore them. Plans without the field load exactly as before (no schema bump); `planStorage` checks takeoffs strictly. Store actions `addTakeoff`, `updateTakeoff` (rename, reference, override, cutouts; null clears), `deleteTakeoff`, `markTakeoffCurrent`: one undo step each. The tool's mode, draft, selected row and Show takeoffs live in `src/store/takeoffStore.ts` and are never saved or undoable.
- **Units.** Metres and m² internally. `src/lib/units/format.ts`: lengths in mm (whole), m (3 decimals) or ft-in (to 1/16", `12'-6 3/16"`, carried); areas in m² (3 decimals), mm² (whole, grouped) or sq ft (3 decimals). **Round once, at display; totals sum the unrounded values** (three 0.6 mm values show "1 mm" each and "2 mm" together). Typed values go through the existing `parseTypedLength` (plus inch fractions turned into decimals); a bare number is in the field's current unit (feet for ft-in). The chosen units live in localStorage "atrium.units" (`src/store/unitsStore.ts`), never in the plan. The area unit is the Summary's m² | sq ft toggle too; the room list and the other panels show m² while mm² is chosen.
- **Status rules (fixed):** with a reference, a length is OK when |measured − reference| ≤ max(2 mm, 0.2% of the reference), an area when ≤ max(0.005 m², 0.4%), else Flagged; no reference is "No reference". **An override never replaces the measured value**: the row shows both ("Overridden: 4.100 m, measured 4.062 m"), the status still compares the measured value, the effective value is the override when there is one, and the CSV and totals give measured, override and effective separately.
- **Basis:** "PDF exact", "Traced approximate", "Drawn" or "Free points": the worst provenance (traced < drawn < PDF) among the walls whose snap points (face corners, wall ends, jambs, midpoints) the points sit on within 1 µm, derived at display (`rows.basisOf`). A PDF wall since edited counts as drawn. A traced basis says "approximate".
- **Points are absolute and do not follow walls.** `wallsHash` (ids, ends and thicknesses of all walls) is stamped when a takeoff is added; when the current hash differs the row says "Walls have moved since this was measured" with "Mark as current" (one undo step).
- **The tool** (`takeoff`, key K): 2D plan only. It is disabled in 3D-only ("Takeoff works in the 2D plan") and while walking, and it steps back to Select on entering walk or when the 2D plan goes away. As with Measure, a press never picks a wall; a drag pans and two fingers pinch. Snapping (`snapTakeoffPoint`): face corners, then centre-line ends, then jambs, then midpoints (within 12 px, 44 px for a finger), else the 0.05 m grid; Alt keeps the point as it is, and Shift keeps it level or plumb with the previous point. Length: click points; double-click, Enter or Finish ends it ("Length N"). Area: click corners; click the first one, Enter or Finish closes it. A crossing outline is refused, and the draft stays. "Add cutout" draws a polygon that must lie inside the area. Wall: the pointer's side picks the left face, right face or centre line (the middle quarter of the thickness, or 3 px). The Left | Centre | Right toggle forces one, which is the touch path. The whole straight run (`wallRun`) is measured from `wallOutline`; Alt measures one piece. Different thicknesses give a "stepped" face, measured as the polyline with its steps. Backspace removes the last point; Escape cancels the draft (history unchanged), and a second Escape goes back to Select. Finish, Undo point and Cancel buttons are the touch path.
- **Panel** (`TakeoffPanel.tsx`, a collapsible section under Rooms): unit switches, Show takeoffs, the table (scrolls inside its own box), totals, Download CSV (`<plan name>-takeoffs.csv`; values in the chosen units, the units in the header, the difference in mm or m²) and Copy table (tab-separated).
- **Limits:** 2D only (nothing in 3D); straight segments only, no curved shapes; no scale recalibration until M.2b; one floor. Overlapping cutouts are each subtracted. Labels of takeoffs drawn on the same line overlap on the canvas. Dev hook: `window.__takeoffStore` (development only).

## One units setting (step U.1)
- **One store.** `src/store/unitsStore.ts` is the only source: `{ length: "mm" | "m" | "ftin", area: "m2" | "mm2" | "sqft" }` (the length key is stored as `"ftin"`; `"ft-in"` is read as the same), in localStorage `"atrium.units"`, default metres and m². The TopBar's **Units** menu (two real radio groups, so Tab and the arrow keys work, and it fits at 390 px), the Summary's area switch, the Takeoff panel's two switches and the import review's dimension tables (mm | m | ft-in) are all views of it: change one and they all change. It is also mirrored into `src/lib/units/current.ts` on every change, so pure code (the edit rules' messages, Push/Pull and Move labels, the validator's warnings, the measure label) uses the same unit without importing a store; in Node nothing sets it and the defaults give the text these messages always had.
- **Display only; storage is metres and m².** The plan, takeoffs and autosave never hold a unit. Components show and read lengths through `useUnitFormat` (`src/components/studio/useUnitFormat.ts`) and `formatLength` / `formatArea` (`src/lib/units/format.ts`); never write a unit into a string. The studio's decimals are `STUDIO_DP`: metres to 2, m² and sq ft to 1 (what the studio has always shown, so default-unit text is unchanged); mm, mm² and ft-in (1/16") have their own rounding; the Takeoff table keeps 3 and 3.
- **Typing.** Number fields show the value in the chosen unit and parse typed input with the existing parser (`parseLengthInput` = `parseTypedLength`, which lives in `src/lib/units/typed.ts` and is re-exported by `importFile.ts`, plus: a **bare number is in the current unit**: metres, mm, or feet in ft-in; anything with a unit or a mark wins, so `12'6` is 3.81 m). Push/Pull's typed distance does the same.
- **A typed value is kept as typed, to 0.1 mm** (step M.1d, `TYPED_STEP = 1e-4` in `src/lib/plan/edit.ts`): typing `13'6"` in a wall's length stores 4.1148 m, not the 4.11 m the old 1 cm rounding gave, and `3800 mm` stores exactly 3.8 m. It applies to `lengthTarget` and `typedTarget` (the wall panel's length and a drawn wall's typed length) and to the opening and item panels' typed fields. **Drags and snaps are unchanged**: `ROUND_STEP` is still 1 cm on release, the draw and opening grids are still 5 cm, and Push/Pull's face edits (`resizeOpeningEdge` / `Head` / `Sill`, `pullWallTop`) still land on 1 cm, because they are drags, not fields.
- **Fields keep their stored values unless the user changes them.** A field shows a rounded value (2.7184 m is "2718 mm"), so `shouldCommit` skips a blur whose text is empty or the text it showed, and each panel skips a typed value equal to the stored one within 1e-9 (`sameLength`). Focusing and leaving a field, pressing Enter on it untouched, retyping its own text, or typing the same value another way adds no undo step and cannot make the stored value drift to its rounded display.
- **Rows are rounded; totals are summed from exact values.** The room list rounds each row on its own; the Summary's total is `formatArea` of the exact sum (74.695 m² shows 74.7 m² while the rows add to 74.6), and a note under the list says "Rows are rounded; the total is summed from exact values." The scale bar uses `niceScaleBarFor`: 1, 2, 2.5, 5 × 10ⁿ in mm or m; 1, 2, 3, 6 inches then 1, 2, 4, 5 × 10ⁿ feet in ft-in; always 60–140 px.
- **Guard test and allow-list.** `scripts/test-units-everywhere.ts` (in `npm test`) scans `src/components`, `src/app` and `src/lib/plan2d` for hard-coded unit text (an expression then m, mm, cm, ft, m², mm², sq ft; a number then a unit; an area sign in a string), skipping comments and `src/lib/units`. A hit must have an entry in `scripts/units-allowlist.json` (`file`, `match` = a substring of the line, `reason`); an entry that matches nothing fails as stale (the list is empty today: nothing in those folders needs an exception). The pure rules in `src/lib/plan` use `lenText` / `areaText` from `src/lib/units/current.ts` instead (not scanned, but converted: when you write a message with a length, use them).
- **Never import `src/app/studio/import/importFile.ts` from `src/lib`.** It starts the analyse and PDF workers (`new Worker(new URL(…))`); a lib module reaching it made a cycle through the PDF worker's bundle (edit.ts → format.ts → importFile.ts → pdf.worker → snap.ts → edit.ts) that panicked and then stalled `next build` for 20+ minutes with no output. Shared pure helpers belong in `src/lib`.
- **Not display units:** the 3D model import dialog's SOURCE unit radios (they describe the file), degrees and percentages, pixels per metre, and the PDF reader's own notes in `src/lib/pdf` (written once at read time, in metres or mm).

## Hinges, typed precision and gap reporting (step M.1d)

- **A typed value keeps 0.1 mm.** `TYPED_STEP = 1e-4` (`src/lib/plan/edit.ts`) is what a typed field rounds to, so `13'6"` stores 4.1148 m and `3800 mm` stores 3.8 m exactly. `lengthTarget` and `typedTarget` use it, as do `OpeningPanel`'s and `ItemPanel`'s typed fields. Drags, snaps and Push/Pull's face edits keep `ROUND_STEP` (1 cm) and their 5 cm grids: a pointer is not a typed number. The U.1 section has the rest.
- **Hinge versus swing: two fields, four states.** `Opening.hinge?: "a" | "b"` says which JAMB the leaf pivots on — the gap end nearer the wall's `a` end, or the one nearer `b`. `Opening.swing` still says which SIDE of the wall it opens to, relative to `a`→`b`. They are independent, so a door has four storable states. **Absent `hinge` means "a"**, exactly what every door did before, so old plans load and draw unchanged (`planStorage` reads only `"a"` and `"b"` and drops anything else; `test-persist.ts` has the assertions). Rooms, collision, takeoffs, `validatePlan` and the schedules all ignore it; `validatePlan` still requires a swing on a door and none on a window, and says nothing about the hinge.
- **One reading of both.** `doorSides` (`src/lib/plan2d/openings.ts`) turns an opening into `{ atA, side }`, and it is the only place either field is interpreted: `doorSwing` (the 2D plan and `pickOpeningAt`'s swing quarter, whose arm now runs hinge → far jamb), and `doorLeaf3d` (`src/lib/plan/meshBuilders.ts`), which gives `PlanModel` the leaf's pivot x, the leaf box's x inside it, and the turn. The turn is `LEAF_OPEN × (hinged at a ? −side : +side)`: a leaf reaching local +x needs a negative turn to swing towards +z (the left normal) and one reaching −x a positive one, so moving the hinge alone both moves the leaf to the other jamb and mirrors its turn.
- **The panel.** `OpeningPanel`'s "Hinge side: A end | B end" sits under Flip swing — a real radio-style group (`data-testid="opening-hinge"`, `data-hinge`, `aria-pressed`, keyboard reachable), one `updateOpening` and so one undo step. `flipDoor` (the Flip button, the Left/Right control and the door tools) touches only `swing` and never the hinge; moving the hinge never touches the swing. `markEdited` counts a hinge change like any other, so a PDF opening then says "Edited".
- **Walls are never turned round.** `snap.ts` keeps every wall running the way `walls.ts` read it (a = the end with the smaller coordinate) and stores `hinge: "b"` for a door the PDF drew on the far jamb. M.1c instead reversed such a wall and mirrored any door left over, with a note; both are gone.
- **A leaf may be a thin closed rectangle.** `rectLeaf` (`src/lib/pdf/openings.ts`) accepts, beside the single line, a whole path of four straight segments forming a closed rectangle: square corners and equal opposite sides to 2% of the radius, 20–100 mm (`ARC_RULES.rectLeaf`) across its short side, as long as the radius within 2%, with one short end's midpoint at the arc's centre and the other's at an arc end. `Arc.leaf` is the list of its segment indexes, and `walls.ts` keeps all four out of the wall faces — a 60 mm leaf would otherwise pair into a thin 0.9 m wall. A rectangle with **no** valid swing arc is never a door on its own: that gap stays unclassified. Thinner than 20 mm, one of its long sides passes the plain line-leaf test instead, so the door is still found.
- **Gaps outside 0.45–4 m are reported** (since step Q.4a, not a face break exactly as wide as a wall meeting it at a T). Behaviour is unchanged — a collinear gap under 0.45 m still closes as solid wall and one over 4 m is still not bridged — but `walls.ts` records each in `VectorWalls.gaps` (page metres, with its size), `snap.ts` carries them to `Coverage.outOfRangeGaps` in plan metres through both the merge and the whole-plan fallback, and the review screen says "N gaps outside 450 mm to 4000 mm were not read as openings (sizes: 300 mm, 4600 mm)" (`coverage-gaps-line`, with `data-sizes` unrounded) and highlights each one on the overlay in its own "Gaps not read" layer. An overlap is not a gap, and neither is a break another wall crosses.
- **Import-cycle guard.** `scripts/test-import-cycles.ts` (in `npm test`) walks `src/` following static imports, dynamic `import()` and `new Worker(new URL(...))` edges, and fails on any cycle, printing its path; it also fails if anything under `src/lib` imports `src/app/studio/import/importFile.ts` (the U.1 rule). `CYCLE_ROOT` points it at another copy of the tree. **Run `npm run build` under a 5-minute cap.** This Mac has no `timeout` and no `gtimeout`, so use `perl -e 'alarm 300; exec @ARGV' npm run build`: a build that takes over 5 minutes means an import cycle or a hang, not a slow machine. A clean build here is about 2 s.
- **Limits of M.1d:** bi-fold, pocket, revolving and arched doors are still not read, and neither is any opening in a diagonal or curved wall. A leaf rectangle thicker than 100 mm is not a leaf, and its two long sides can then pair as a thin wall. The hinge is not editable in 3D (Push/Pull and Move do not touch it) and there is no 2D handle for it: use the panel. `LEAF_OPEN` is a fixed 0.3 rad (about 17°) with no dev flag, so a 3D screenshot shows the leaf only slightly open. No real CAD PDF has been read.

## Recalibrating, PDF dimension rows, phantom openings (step M.2b)
- **Recalibration scales plan-space lengths about the origin.** `src/lib/plan/calibrate.ts` is pure: `calibrateFactor(p1, p2, trueDistance)` = trueDistance / |p1 − p2|, refused when the points are under 0.1 m apart, the distance isn't positive, or the factor is outside 0.1–10; `scalePlan(plan, factor, origin = (0, 0))` multiplies wall endpoints and thicknesses, opening offsets and widths, takeoff points and cutouts, and imported items' plan positions. **Not scaled:** wall heights, opening sills and heights, an imported model's own size, height and rotation, and typed references, overrides and notes. `calibrationProblem` refuses the result when it fails `validatePlan`, when an opening no longer fits its wall's usable span (`openingProblem`), or when a wall would be under 0.2 m; `planStore.calibratePlan` checks first, so a refusal changes nothing, not even history. It is ONE undo step and redo works, and it is the one edit that asks `edit()` to keep the provenance marks (`keepMarks`): a uniform rescale is not an edit of one wall's shape against the drawing, so nothing is marked "Edited". A takeoff that was current stays current (its `wallsHash` is re-stamped in the same action). `plan.calibrations?: { factor, note? }[]` records each applied change, with no timestamps so saves stay deterministic. Old plans have no such field and load unchanged.
- **The flow.** "Set scale…" is in the Takeoff panel and beside the 2D scale bar. `src/store/calibrateStore.ts` holds the picking (never in the plan, never saved); it is not a tool, so while it is picking `PlanCanvas` handles the press before any tool and no wall, opening or model is picked, hovered or dragged (a drag still pans, two fingers still pinch). Points snap exactly as a takeoff's do, Alt turns snapping off, Escape cancels with history untouched. Starting it opens the 2D pane the way picking a 2D tool does. `CalibrateDialog.tsx` is a native `<dialog>` with `showModal()` (focus trapped, Escape closes); it shows the measured distance, a field read by the existing parser, a live preview ("Factor 1.0200 (+2.00%). Plan 10.00 × 8.00 m becomes 10.20 × 8.16 m. Walls, openings and takeoffs scale; heights and imported models do not."), a warning beyond 0.8–1.25, and Apply / Cancel. After Apply the 2D camera fits the plan.
- **PDF dimension rows are takeoffs with source "pdf".** At Build model, every dimension the review matched to a line becomes a length takeoff (`src/lib/pdf/takeoffs.ts`): the two measured ends in plan metres, `reference` = the printed value with the printed text as its note, any review override carried with its note, `source: "pdf"`, and `approximate: true` when the model-versus-printed check landed it on a traced wall. The ends of a printed dimension line lie off the building, so no point of such a row sits ON a wall: its basis is "PDF exact", or "Traced approximate" from that carried flag — never derived from the points. The status rules, units, overrides, stale marker and CSV are the takeoffs' own, unchanged; the CSV gains a last column, the source ("pdf" or "studio"). The panel's "From PDF" group is collapsible with its count and "N flagged". A calibration changes these rows' measured values, so a row that flags after Set scale is the printed numbers disagreeing with the new scale — that is the signal, and the panel says so. Capped at 200 rows (`MAX_PDF_TAKEOFFS`), with the cap said on the review screen and in the panel. Scanned PDFs and images carry nothing.
- **Phantom traced openings are dropped on vector pages.** In `snap.ts`, a traced opening with no vector opening within 3 raster pixels and under 0.45 m wide is dropped when one contiguous run of the PDF's own walls covers its span with no PDF opening in it — the PDF draws solid wall there (walls.ts has already closed any sub-0.45 m break as wall). An opening on a wall that is itself traced never reaches that test and stays traced as before. They are reported in `Coverage.droppedOpenings` and on the review screen: "N traced openings dropped because the PDF draws solid wall there (sizes: 300 mm)." Image input is unchanged.
- **Limits of M.2b:** one uniform factor, no per-axis correction and no rotation; the scale is taken about the plan's origin only (there is no "keep this point fixed"); a calibration does not re-run the PDF reading, so the walls keep the shapes they were read with; the From PDF rows' measured values follow the plan, not the drawing, so re-importing is still the way to change what was read; no real CAD PDF has been read.

## The e2e suites and the dev-only hooks (step F.1)

- **Every suite in `scripts/` shares `scripts/e2e-helpers.ts`**: `warmRoutes` (ask for the routes the suite needs and check they answered, before the first browser), `waitForHook` / `waitUntil` / `waitForScene3d` (named ready conditions whose message says what was missing, where and for how long), `step()` (printed, so a hang or a timeout says how far it got), `withSuiteTimeout` and `watchErrors` (the one console and page-error collector; with `E2E_LOG_ERRORS=1` it prints each one as it arrives instead of only at the final assertion).
- **Never sleep before reading a hook, a mesh or anything the 3D scene installs.** The store hooks (`__planStore`, `__selectionStore`, `__toolStore`, `__pushPullDebug`, `__takeoffStore`, `__calibrateStore`) are set when their module is evaluated, so they are there as soon as the page's JavaScript has run. The four scene hooks (`__scene3d`, `__studio3d`, `__walkDebug`, `__importDebug`) are set by effects INSIDE the react-three-fiber tree, which `<Canvas>` renders into a SECOND React root, from an effect, after its renderer promise resolves — they cannot be installed before first render, and they are deleted when that tree unmounts, which every switch to the 2D-only view does. Wait for them by name (`waitForScene3d`, which also waits for ViewCamera to move the camera off react-three-fiber's default `(0, 0, 5)`), never for a number of milliseconds. A blind wait is still fine for what it actually says (a 2D refit, OrbitControls damping, proving a debounce does NOT fire).
- **The hooks are development-only** (`process.env.NODE_ENV === "development"`), so no suite can run against `npm run start`; against a production server every one of them fails at its first hook read. That is the guarantee they are never shipped, and the dev-hook grep says the same.
- **A function passed to `page.evaluate`, `page.waitForFunction` or `addInitScript` must not contain a NAMED inner function.** tsx compiles these scripts with esbuild's keepNames, which wraps `const hide = () => …` as `__name(() => …, "hide")`; Playwright injects only the function's source, so the page throws `__name is not defined` — and when the page's own JavaScript dies with it, taps do nothing and navigations never happen. Pass a string (`addInitScript({ content: "…" })`, `waitForFunction("…")`) or keep the injected function free of named declarations.
- **`scripts/flake-hunt.ts`** is the on-demand tool for this: it runs every suite many rounds against a freshly restarted dev server, a long-lived warm one and a production build, records each failure with its step and its first console error, and never retries. See the F.1 findings in docs/HANDOFF.md.

## Self-hosted OCR, no third-party requests (step O.1)
- **No third-party network requests at runtime.** Nothing the app does may contact a CDN or any host but its own origin. Files never leave the browser.
- **OCR assets are served from `/tesseract/`** (`public/tesseract/`, about 11 MB: `worker.min.js`, the SIMD and non-SIMD `*-lstm.wasm.js` cores, `eng.traineddata.gz`, and `LICENSES.md`), from tesseract.js 5.1.1 and tesseract.js-core 5.1.1 with `4.0.0_best_int` English data (what the CDN defaults served). `src/lib/blueprint/ocrAssets.ts` is the one place that names them: `ocrOptions()` gives `createWorker` its `workerPath`, `corePath` and `langPath` (absolute URLs in a page or worker, plain paths under Node), so tests read the same files. tesseract.js's own defaults are CDN URLs: never call `createWorker` without `ocrOptions()`.
- **Errors:** a missing or blocked OCR file gives "The OCR files could not be loaded: <file>" (`missingOcrFile` probes each file after a failure); a worker that did not start says "The OCR worker failed to start".
- **CDN guard:** `scripts/test-ocr-assets.ts` (in `npm test`) fails on any CDN URL (jsdelivr, unpkg, cdnjs, googleapis, gstatic, projectnaptha) in `src/`, on any `workerPath`/`corePath`/`langPath` literal outside `/tesseract/`, on a `createWorker` without `ocrOptions()`, and on a file `ocrAssets.ts` names that is missing from `public/tesseract/` or its `LICENSES.md`.
- **Bundle:** tesseract.js's own CDN default is blanked at build time by `scripts/tesseract-default-options-loader.cjs` (`next.config.ts`), so `grep -rlE "cdn\.jsdelivr|unpkg\.com|cdnjs" .next/static` must print nothing.
- **"Works offline" holds only while `scripts/e2e-offline-ocr.ts` passes** (not in `npm test`; run before committing). It imports an image and `plan-outlined.pdf` at 1440 and 390 with every non-local request blocked, then in a browser that cannot resolve any host but localhost, and asserts zero blocked attempts.

## The drawing under the 2D plan, and Rescaled labels (step M.2c)
- **The background is a view of the original drawing, never geometry.** `plan.background?: { assetId, kind: "pdf" | "image", page?, mapping }` (`src/types/plan.ts`). Nothing reads walls, openings or rooms from it; it is never picked, hovered or snapped to. Walls, rooms, `validatePlan`, collision, walk and the takeoff maths ignore it. Store actions `setBackground` and `clearBackground` are one undo step each; Remove drawing in the panel clears it and keeps the file in the asset store, as in I.1a.
- **The mapping is affine, from the drawing's own units to plan metres**, in SVG matrix order (x′ = a·x + c·y + e, y′ = b·x + d·y + f). The units are PDF points (the page viewport at scale 1, /Rotate applied, y down) or image pixels (y down, after the photo's own EXIF turn). It is captured at Build model by `buildMapping` (`src/lib/background/mapping.ts`) from the numbers the build used. A vector build (snap.ts, its fallback included) gives `pt × Coverage.mPerPt − Coverage.origin`. Everything else gives the image pipeline's chain: units → original raster (× pxPerPt for a PDF page, `PdfInfo.pageSize` gives its size) → `analyse.ts`'s downscale (each axis by its own rounded ratio) → `deskew.ts`'s rotation → `(px − origin) / pxPerM`. Pixel coordinates are continuous, with a pixel's centre at index + 0.5, as `vectorize.ts`'s are: no half-pixel shift.
- **Calibration scales the mapping.** `scalePlan` applies `scaleAbout(mapping, factor, origin)`, so the drawing stays under the walls it described; `calibratePlan` writes it in its one undo step.
- **Bytes live in IndexedDB, never in the autosave.** At Build model `keepDrawing` (`src/lib/background/keep.ts`) puts the picked file (the whole PDF, or the image) through the I.1a asset store, with the same SHA-256 id rule. Storage that is off, blocked or full still completes the import, with the note "The drawing could not be kept for the background." and no `background`. The autosave holds the descriptor only. `planStorage` drops a malformed descriptor and keeps the plan. A missing asset draws nothing, and the panel says "…Import it again to see the background."
- **Opacity and visibility are view settings**: localStorage `"atrium.background"` `{ visible, opacity }` (`src/store/backgroundStore.ts`, default shown at 50%). They are never in the plan, its history or the autosave. The 2D pane (beside Set scale…) and the plan panel's Drawing section both show the same "Drawing" toggle and opacity slider (`DrawingControls.tsx`): native button and range input, 44 px tall. Neither is shown in the 3D-only view.
- **Rendering** (`src/components/plan2d/BackgroundLayer.tsx`): an element BEHIND the `<svg>` (which is `relative`, so everything in the plan paints above it), placed by one CSS matrix: camera ∘ mapping ∘ bitmap placement.
  - An image is the `<img>` itself.
  - A PDF page is rendered by pdf.js in `src/workers/background.worker.ts`, with the same safety options and wasm path as `pdf.worker.ts`. It renders only the visible region, at device pixels, capped at 4096 px on the longer side (`regionFor`). The render is asked for once the camera, pane or mapping has been still for 300 ms (`SETTLE_MS`), never per frame. The previous bitmap stays up, correctly placed, until the new one arrives. A newer request cancels the running RenderTask, late replies are ignored by id, and the same region is never asked for twice.
  - Dev only: `window.__background = { kind, mapping, bitmapSize, region, renderCount, pending }`, guarded by `NODE_ENV !== "production"`, so it is in the dev-hook grep.
- **Rescaled labels.** While `plan.calibrations` is non-empty, `rescaledText` (`calibrate.ts`) gives "Rescaled x1.0234", the product of every factor to 4 decimals. "Read from PDF" and "Traced, approximate" in the wall and opening panels, the "PDF exact" basis cell and the From PDF heading add it after " · ". "Edited" stays exactly "Edited". Undoing every calibration removes it.
- **Limits:**
  - One background per plan, and one PDF page (the page that was read).
  - No snapping to the drawing yet: takeoff points snap to the plan's walls, which the drawing sits under.
  - The claim is **no third-party requests**, not "works offline": the drawing needs only the browser's own storage and our own `/pdfjs/wasm/`.
  - An image whose EXIF turn the browser does not apply to an `<img>` would be drawn turned.
  - A huge zoom-out renders the whole page at under 4096 px, so it can look soft until you zoom in.
  - Panning shows blank paper beyond the last rendered region until the 300 ms settle.

## Labels never overprint (step L.1)
- **Every plan label goes through the layout pass.** Room names and areas, takeoff labels (plain, selected and carried from a PDF) and imported models' names are built as data (`src/lib/plan2d/labels.ts`, `buildLabels`) and placed by the pure `layoutLabels` (`src/lib/plan2d/labelLayout.ts`), in screen pixels. Do not draw a new label with a bare `<text>` at its natural spot: build it as a `PlanLabel`, draw it with `LabelText`, and give it a kind. A label's position is stored as an OFFSET from its plan point, so a pan carries it. Each label's rectangle is estimated (`estimateWidth`: 0.6 em a glyph, `labelHeight`), never measured; `e2e-labels.ts` checks the real rectangle always fits inside the reserved one.
- **Placement.** Highest priority first, ties by id, so the result never depends on input order. For each label: its natural centre, then above, below, left, right and the four diagonals at 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5 and 3 label heights, nearest first; the first rectangle that is inside the pane (2 px padding) and free wins; a label that moved more than half its height gets a leader line (from its plan point to the nearest edge of its rectangle); one with nowhere to go is HIDDEN. A label wholly outside the pane is `offscreen`, which is not hidden. The scale bar, a placed Measure value and the plan's controls are obstacles: `fixed`, placed exactly where they are, never moved, clamped or hidden.
- **Controls are obstacles (step L.1b).** The zoom buttons, "Set scale…", the Labels control, its "N hidden" and the Drawing group each carry `data-plan-obstacle="…"`. `PlanCanvas` measures every such element relative to the pane in a layout effect (so a label never paints under a button first) and again whenever the pane or one of them changes size (`ResizeObserver`), and `controlObstacles` (`labels.ts`) hands them to `layoutLabels` as kind `control`, priority 100, each grown by `PAN_KEEP_PX` on every side, because a pan of up to that much keeps the last layout and a label may drift that far. A label that cannot avoid one is hidden and counted; a selected takeoff is forced, as always. A new floating control must carry the attribute, or labels will sit under it. The Measure, Wall, Door/Window and Set scale hint bars and the drag message are NOT obstacles.
- **Priorities (`PRIORITY`, highest first):** live read-outs and controls 100; the selected takeoff and a selected model 90; the scale bar 85; a selected wall's length 80; takeoffs 70; room areas 60; room names 50; carried PDF dimensions 40; unselected models 35; unselected wall labels 30; opening labels 20. **At or above 90, and the scale bar, a label is never hidden**: if nothing is free it stays on its natural spot, overlapping (`forced`). Wall length, selected-wall and opening labels are not drawn today; their slots are reserved, nothing builds them.
- **When it runs.** Never per frame. The layout is computed from the camera after it has been still for `SETTLE_MS` (BackgroundLayer's debounce), at once after a fit, and again when the plan, the units, the selection, the takeoffs or the Labels setting change. A pan of `PAN_KEEP_PX` (12 px) or less at the same scale keeps the last layout (`keepsLayout`): the greedy layout is not continuous where a label is clamped against the pane's edge, so without this a 3 px pan could jump a label 8 px. While a drag read-out is live (`hud`) the last layout is kept, and only the live label moves. Pointer-following read-outs (drag length, draw length, snap word, takeoff live total, wall-hover lines, door and window preview) are drawn on top and are not in the pass; the placed Measure value is. `data-labels-settled`, `-hidden`, `-placed` and `-detail` on the canvas and, in development only, `window.__plan2d.labels` say what the pass did.
- **The Labels setting is a view setting, never plan data.** Full, Compact (room areas, takeoffs and the selected model; no room names, no carried PDF dimensions, no unselected models) and Off (only the selected takeoff and the selected model). `src/store/labelsStore.ts`, localStorage `"atrium.labels"` `{ detail }`; a missing or garbled entry means "never chosen". With the Drawing visible and nothing ever chosen the setting in force is Compact, without the drawing Full (`effectiveDetail`); a default is never written, and once the user chooses, their choice stays whatever the drawing does. The control is a native 44 px `<select>` in the 2D pane beside "Set scale…" (`LabelControls.tsx`). It must stay narrow: that row floats over the plan, and a 215 px-wide segmented version covered a wall a test taps at 390.
- **Hidden labels are counted, not lost.** `LayoutResult.hidden` lists their ids (labels, not rooms: a small room in Full counts twice, its name and its area); the pane shows "N hidden" beside the control exactly when N > 0, as its own `pointer-events-none` element (never a tap target, so a wider control can't cover the plan), and the Labels select's tooltip says why (overprinting, under a control, or a room too small). A hidden label comes back as soon as there is room (zoom in, another setting, a takeoff deleted); a selected takeoff's label never goes. A room too small on screen to hold its label (the older fit rule in `buildLabels`) is built with `tooSmall` and hidden BY THE PASS, so it is counted too (step L.1b); one off screen is `offscreen`, not hidden.
- **Every label has a backing plate (step L.1b):** the SVG filter `#label-plate` (defined in PlanCanvas's svg, applied by `LabelText`) floods the text's own box with the pane's `limestone` token at 80%, so the drawing under the plan does not read through a label (measured: at most 7/255 through a label with the drawing at 100%, 140/255 without). It is a filter, not a `<rect>` beside the text, so `plan-room-label` still has exactly [name, area] as children and the layout's rectangles are unchanged. Chromium paints a filter's flood even under `visibility: hidden`, so a test that hides the plan to see only the drawing must also hide `[data-label]` (e2e-background does).
- **Long values.** A label over `MAX_LINE_PX` (240 px) wraps onto two lines at its colon (the value never breaks), else at the middle space; a line still too long is shortened with an ellipsis. The whole text goes in a `<title>` tooltip on a wrapped or shortened label (it takes pointer events for that; the plan's own handlers still decide what a press hits), and it is always in the Takeoff panel. Units are the one units setting; a mm² value is in the label (14,822,500 mm²).
- **The Takeoff mode bar is a strip above the canvas, not over it** (`TakeoffBar` inside `PlanCanvas`): the canvas gets shorter. Its height changes with the mode and the pane's width (a narrow pane wraps its buttons), so whenever it changes, from or to zero too, the camera shifts by exactly that amount, in the same commit and before the paint: the plan stays where it is on the page, and the resize that follows is not refitted (`shiftForStrip`, `TakeoffBar`'s `onHeight`). Existing tests aim at the plan across a mode switch and a tool change and rely on that. The Measure, Wall, Door, Window and Set scale hint bars are still overlays.
- **Limits:** no per-label dragging; 2D only (3D labels are untouched). A label is placed against other labels and controls, not against walls, the dashed takeoff lines or the drawing's own printed text (the plate hides what is behind a label's box, but the drawing's large room names still show around a small plate). While a pan is in progress, before it settles, a label travels with the plan and can pass under a control. Widths are estimates. Obstacles are re-measured on resize and content changes; a control that moves without changing size and without a resize of the pane would be missed. Not tested on a real phone, Safari or Firefox.

## Snapping to the drawing (step P.1)
- **A worker-built index from the original PDF.** When the Takeoff tool first shows with a PDF `plan.background`, `src/store/drawingSnapStore.ts` reads the file's bytes through the background store (the I.1a asset store). A separate instance of `src/workers/background.worker.ts` then extracts the page's VectorPage and builds `src/lib/pdf/snapIndex.ts`'s index there (message `index`), never on the main thread, and is terminated after its reply. The index is cached per asset id and page for the session and released when the background is cleared or replaced. While it loads, the Takeoff bar says "Reading drawing for snapping…", and clicks use the walls' snaps; nothing waits. An image background, a scanned page, a missing file or a failed worker give "Drawing snapping needs a vector PDF". It is view state: never in the plan, its history or the autosave.
- **Points are stored in PDF points** (corners: segment ends meeting within 0.05 pt; ends; straight segments' midpoints; and the segments themselves, for the nearest point on a line). They are converted to plan metres with `plan.background.mapping` at query time, so a calibration, which scales the mapping, moves them with no rebuild. A grid of 5 mm cells answers a query from the cells within its reach (50k segments: 1000 queries in about 5 ms). At most 200,000 points are kept (corners, then ends, then midpoints), with a note in the bar.
- **Clutter is skipped:** segments under 1 mm on paper; stroked lines under 0.1 pt (filled edges have width 0 and are kept); anything in a 5 mm cell holding more than 40 segments; hatching (4+ parallel lines of one length and stroke, overlapping, under 5 mm apart, evenly spaced, plus every other line of that exact family); and points buried strictly inside a NARROW filled path (box at most 5 mm across: a wall's poché), such as a partition's end drawn on into the wall it meets. Wide fills (a page background) bury nothing.
- **Priority and tolerance** (`snapTakeoffPoint`): wall face corners → the drawing's corners and line ends (the nearer of the two) → centre-line ends → jambs → wall midpoints → the drawing's midpoints → the nearest point on a drawing line → the 5 cm grid. The walls keep 12 px (`SNAP_PX`); the drawing reaches 14 px (`DRAWING_PX`); a finger gets 44 px for both. Alt turns all snapping off; Shift's level/plumb still applies after snapping. The marker's word is "corner", "end", "mid" or "edge" (`SnapKind` `drawing-*`), a live read-out like the other snap words. Set scale's own point picking does NOT use the drawing.
- **The snap kind is stored per point:** `Takeoff.drawingSnaps?: (DrawingSnapKind | null)[]`, one per point, written only when at least one point snapped to the drawing. A Shift-held point is stored as null. Cutout points are not tracked. Points stay absolute metres. A takeoff whose every point snapped to the drawing (and has no cutouts) is "PDF exact" wherever the walls are (`rows.drawnExact`), with " · Rescaled …" after a calibration as before. `planStorage` drops a malformed list and keeps the takeoff; old plans load unchanged.
- **Limits:** vector PDFs only (a scanned page or an image has nothing to snap to); one page, the one the plan was read from; curves only as their flattened segments (no true arc centres, tangents or intersections); line–line intersections that are not segment ends are not snap points; no per-point dragging of a finished takeoff; the cap's 200,000 points; stair treads may be read as hatching. Tested on synthetic fixtures only: no real CAD PDF.

## Pixel budget, content box and worker errors (step X.1)
- **The cause of X.1 was not a raster.** `polylabel` (`src/lib/plan/rooms.ts`) sized its first grid by the polygon's shorter side. A closed loop whose walls are as thick as the loop is deep insets to a sliver about 1e-15 m tall, which asked for about 6e11 cells. That ran on the import page's main thread (the review build, `buildPdfPlan` → `deriveRooms`) and the tab died with no error. A polygon thinner than the precision now returns its box's centre at once. Pure geometry that allocates by a ratio of two lengths must bound that ratio.
- **The pixel budget.** No raster or canvas is over `MAX_EDGE` (4096 px a side) or `PIXEL_BUDGET` (4096², about 16.8 MP), both in `src/lib/pdf/render.ts`.
  - Callers size their renders under it: `scanRasterScale` (a scan, or outlined text for OCR, at 300 dpi held to the budget; before X.1 it allowed 40 MP), `vectorRaster` (under 2400 px a side), BackgroundLayer's `MAX_SIDE` (= `MAX_EDGE`), and `decodeImage` (an image over the budget is scaled down before any canvas).
  - `renderPage` and the background worker refuse anything over it before allocating, `renderPage` with the plain message `tooBigToRender`. A worker that runs out of memory takes the whole tab with it, so the budget is checked BEFORE the canvas exists.
- **The content box.** A vector page whose drawing (`contentBox`: strokes grown by half their width, text turned with its rotation, plus a 10 mm margin, clipped to the page) spans under `CROP_BELOW` (0.5) of the sheet's longer side renders only that box. The resolution is chosen for the box (100 px/m under the 2400 px cap), in whole pixels of the full page's raster, so the crop's pixels are the full render's.
  - Every other page renders whole, exactly as before X.1. On an A4 plan the box is most of the sheet, and a crop would only change the tracing every fixture's results were read from.
  - `PdfInfo.rasterOrigin` (points) and `rasterSize` (pixels) carry the crop; absent means the whole page. A raster pixel is `(point − rasterOrigin) × pxPerPt`. Every point ↔ pixel conversion goes through it: `pdfLabels` and `toScaleEstimate` (analysePdf.ts), snap.ts's traced origin, the fallback's origin and the naming origin, `planToPixels` (the review overlay, which now takes `rasterOrigin`), and `buildMapping`'s raster route. A new conversion must too.
- **Caps with plain messages** (`extract.ts`): 500,000 path commands (`MAX_PATH_OPS`, counted before any work), 500,000 flattened segments (`MAX_SEGMENTS`: a few curve commands can flatten into millions), and 50,000 text items (`MAX_TEXT_ITEMS`).
- **Every worker failure reaches the page in plain words.** That covers an error the worker reports, `onerror`, `onmessageerror`, and silence: `runAnalysis` and `runPdf` (`importFile.ts`) stop a worker that sends nothing for `STALL_MS` (180 s) while it owes an answer, with "Reading this plan stopped responding…" (the PDF page picker's wait does not count). BackgroundLayer treats a dead worker like a failed render. New workers must handle all four.
- **Tests:** `scripts/test-pdf-budget.ts` (in `npm test`) covers:
  - the content box;
  - the budget on A4 to A0 either way up, and `renderPage`'s refusal;
  - `plan-small-on-big.pdf` (plan-filled at 1:100 on an empty A0 sheet): cropped, 96 px/m, every wall within 0.5 mm, every traced opening snapped, rooms named, the overlay and the traced mapping exact through the crop;
  - every vector fixture's PDF walls within 0.5 mm;
  - a 500,001-segment page, 100 curves flattening past the cap, and too many text items, each refused with its message.

  `test-rooms.ts` has the sliver loop. `e2e-pdf-import` fails a crashed tab with "page crashed while importing <file> at stage <stage>".

## General CAD conventions in the PDF reader (step Q.1)
- **Never tune a rule to one file.** Every rule below is a general drawing convention, proven on its own SYNTHETIC fixture that reproduces one class of failure (`scripts/test-pdf-conventions.ts`, failing first). `plan-chatgpt-flat.pdf` (ChatGPT-generated, NOT real CAD) is read with them and tested with honest expectations (`scripts/test-pdf-chatgpt.ts`); its trimmed truth and the truth's own discrepancies are in `tests/fixtures/pdf/plan-chatgpt-flat.truth.json`. No real CAD PDF has been read.
- **1. A stated unit** (`scale.ts` `statedUnit`): a text line such as "ALL DIMENSIONS IN mm", "DIMENSIONS ARE IN CENTIMETRES" or "UNITS: m" makes every bare number on a dimension line a dimension in that unit (rows, samples, the scale). mm, cm and m only: a feet-and-inches note never does, and with no note (or two that disagree) bare numbers stay in the "Feet or metres?" route. The scale's reason names the note. Fixture: plan-bare-mm.
- **2. Dimensions are annotation** (`PdfScale.annotation`, used by `walls.ts`): the line of every dimension whose sample agrees with its band within 5% (with or without a unit), and its extension lines (perpendicular, an end within 0.5 text heights of the line's end, NOT crossing it), are never wall faces. A wall face passing through a dimension line's end is never one. Fixture: plan-dims-close.
- **3. No line runs through a wall** (`walls.ts` `throughWall`): two faces don't pair when a line between them, not on their centre line, pairs with one of them (0.05 m or more apart) beyond either end of their overlap: it is another wall's face, and the pair straddles it. A centre line (midway, within 1 mm) is exempt however far it runs. Fixture: plan-against-wall. KNOWN FAILURES (asserted and printed): an off-centre line along a whole wall and past its ends costs that wall; a centre line collinear with a window's middle glazing line joins it into one long line, so the window is not read.
- **4. A gap is closed only across the whole wall** (`mergeLines` `crosses`): a parallel piece stops a door or window gap being bridged only when its band covers both of the wall's faces. Fixture: plan-window-board (one line in front of a window: a window board or curtain track).
- **5. A swing ends at the strike jamb**: M.1b's leaf rule (a line up to 1.5 m with an end on a curve's end) applies only to curves that are not a recognised arc; it used to throw away the short wall face beginning where a swing arc ends. Fixture: plan-strike-jamb (two doors 0.8 m apart, a leaf drawn shut).
- **9. A face bounds a wall on one side only** (`oneSided`, added in Q.1 beyond the agreed list): where two candidate pairs use one line from OPPOSITE sides over a common stretch, the longer is the wall and the shorter is dropped before the widest-wins selection (equal lengths keep both). Fixture: plan-against-wall.
- **6 and 7. Furniture**, after the joints (`furnitureOf`): rooms are traced on the joined pieces. Rule 6: a small loop's walls (inside under 1 m² or under 0.6 m wide, twice the label point's distance to its edges) are dropped unless the wall's own straight run (the piece it was split from) carries on past the small loops; then walls joined only to dropped walls, repeatedly. Rule 7: a group joined to nothing else (ends or T-junctions within 1 cm) lying wholly inside the biggest group's box is an island. The joints are then made again without them, so a wall furniture had split is whole. Fixture: plan-furniture.
- **Nothing is dropped silently.** `VectorWalls.furniture` lists every dropped group ("loop", "island", step S.1's "treads", and M.1b's clutter pieces as "small") with its footprint as read; `Coverage.furniture` carries it in plan metres through the merge and the fallback; the review screen lists each one with its size and place (`coverage-furniture`, `data-w`, `data-d`, `data-x`, `data-y`). A free-standing 0.5 m column is reported, not lost. Known limits: a real free-standing wall inside the building is dropped as an island, and a real duct or closet under 1 m² by rule 6, both reported; furniture standing end-on against a wall stays as a stub wall (asserted).
- **8. A complete PDF reading stands on its own** (`snap.ts` `buildPdfPlan`): when the merge did not use the PDF's walls as read (it kept a traced wall, left PDF walls out, or fell back), the PDF's walls are merged again with NO traced walls; if that plan has every PDF wall, a clean validator, no free ends and at least one room, it is used, `Coverage.pdfOnly` is true and the review says `PDF_ONLY_LINE` ("Walls read entirely from the PDF; the picture tracing was not used."). A merge that already used every PDF wall is kept as it was. plan-traced-wall (a wall drawn as a picture; plan-single-line until step S.1 read its one-line wall) still merges. Fixtures: plan-dims-close and plan-window-board, whose tracing fuses annotation near the walls into walls tens of mm off.
- **Fixtures stay byte-identical.** New fixture options in `make-pdf-fixtures.ts` default off; regenerating must leave every older PDF unchanged (check with md5 before and after).

## More CAD conventions in the PDF reader (step Q.2)
- **The holdout rule.** A file used to FIND a fix is never used to PROVE it. Every rule below is a general drawing convention proven failing-first on its own SYNTHETIC fixture (`scripts/test-pdf-poche.ts`); `plan-chatgpt-poche.pdf` (ChatGPT-generated poche, NOT real CAD; truth and discrepancies in `plan-chatgpt-poche.truth.json`) is only read with them and tested with honest expectations (`scripts/test-pdf-chatgpt-poche.ts`, Node; its e2e row is Chromium's, which traces differently). After each rule: the whole PDF suite and a snapshot diff of every older fixture's reading and build; fixtures regenerate byte-identical.
- **1. A paper-coloured fill is a mask** (`extract.ts`): a fill-only path whose colour has every channel ≥ 250 gives no segments; filled white AND stroked keeps the stroke only. pdf.js hands every fill colour over as `setFillRGBColor` hex. Fixture: plan-poche-mask.
- **2. Dashed lines are never faces** (`extract.ts` marks a stroke-only path with a dash pattern `dashed`; `walls.ts` leaves them out of faces and out of `classifyOpening`'s lines). Centre, hidden, overhead and setting-out lines are dashed; a cut face is continuous. Fixture: plan-centre-lines.
- **3. A dimension's text sits at the middle of its line** (`scale.ts` `dimensionLine`): of candidate lines within 0.05 text heights of each other across, the one whose middle is nearest the text's centre. Fixture: plan-dims-chain.
- **3b. One wrong dimension is an outlier** (`scale.ts` `combineScale`, OUTLIER): when the band is not good but at least 5 readings agree within 5% and exactly ONE does not, and it is under 20% of all, the scale is the agreeing group's and "good" (title-block check as usual); the wrong one is a flagged row. Never "good" from this with fewer than 5 agreeing; no other scale rule changed. Fixture: plan-dims-one-wrong.
- **4. Clip paths apply** (`extract.ts` CLIPPING): `W`/`W*` add a region (edges + fill rule) to the graphics state; every segment is cut to the parts inside all regions in force. A clip with no geometry clips nothing; text and images are not clipped. `walkPath` is the one path walker for painted and clip paths. **4b:** a diagonal pair is hatching when its middle, or each of its two lines, is inside an axis wall (`countDiagonal`). Fixture: plan-poche-hatch; a synthetic hatched L.
- **5. Rule 3 sees filled walls' edges** (`walls.ts` `pairFaces(faces, edges)`): a filled rectangle's edges never pair again, but a pair straddling one is refused like any other. Fixture: plan-finish-line.
- **6. A sheet holds more than the building (rule 7b)** (`walls.ts` `furnitureOf`, `titleEvidence`): a group joined to nothing in the building and wholly outside its box is dropped ONLY on evidence (title-block words inside it: scale, drawing, project, sheet, issue, rev, date, a 1:N …; or within 10 mm on paper of the sheet's border, the biggest rectangle covering half the page, or of the page edge) and KEPT otherwise. Either way it is in `VectorWalls.detached` / `Coverage.detached` and the review lists it and outlines it on the overlay ("Detached groups" layer) with its size. The building is the biggest BOX among groups with no evidence. Fixtures: plan-title-block (dropped), plan-outbuilding (a 4 × 3 m shed, kept).
- **7. A window's own lines are never a wall** (`walls.ts` `mergeLines` `glazing`): a pair within a bridged opening's span with one line inside the wall's band (a glazing line and the sill in front). Fixture: plan-window-sill.
- **8. A wall closes a gap only by passing through it** (`walls.ts` `crosses`): a perpendicular piece blocks the bridge only when its DRAWN span crosses the wall's whole thickness. An opening a joint then cuts is not read and is in `VectorWalls.conflicts` / `Coverage.conflicts`; the review names it with `snap.conflictText` ("Window 1 (1.20 m) conflicts with a wall ending inside it; not read.") and boxes it in the "Gaps not read" layer. Never dropped silently. Fixture: plan-stem-in-window.
- **9. A filled rectangle is a wall at any angle** (`walls.ts` `diagonalRect`, `joinDiagonals`): its ends move to the crossing with the axis walls they meet, free axis ends move onto its centre line, T-junctions split (a cut opening goes to `conflicts`); `furnitureOf` takes diagonals into rooms and groups but never drops them. Stroked diagonal pairs are still only counted. Fixture: plan-poche-diagonal.
- **10. Rule 8b** (`snap.ts` `buildPdfPlan`, `mergeVector`'s `keepAll`): when rule 8 fails, the PDF's walls are merged with no rounds and no fallback; that plan is used only when it keeps EVERY PDF wall and beats the merged plan by `pdfAloneWins` (step Q.2b, below). `Coverage.freeEnds` (every coverage has it) feeds the review's "N free wall ends remain" line. Fixture: plan-free-end (with a deliberately poor tracing; as traced it ties and the merge stands).
- **test-plans baseline.** `tests/fixtures/pdf/test-plans-baseline.json` holds only numbers per local test-plans/ file (walls exact / traced, openings exact / traced, unclassified, dimensions, flagged in each table, rooms), written from the CHROMIUM e2e run with `E2E_WRITE_BASELINE=1`. `e2e-pdf-import.ts` FAILS when any is worse; a file with no baseline is reported. Never commit the PDFs themselves.

## Merge choice, conflicts and level notes (step Q.2b)
- **The merge choice** (`snap.ts` `pdfAloneWins`, pure, on `PlanTally { kept, problems, rooms }`): rule 8b compares the merged plan with the PDF's walls alone in this order: more PDF walls KEPT, then fewer validator problems, then more rooms; a full tie keeps the merge. A PDF wall is kept when it is in the plan or a traced wall of its group stands in for it (`PdfBuild.kept`); only a wall left out with nothing in its place is lost. So a merge whose validator rounds dropped walls to come out clean never beats a complete reading with a real free end, and a plan keeping under 70% of the PDF's walls never beats one keeping all (implied by comparing walls first). Counting only exact walls would flip plan-free-end (15 exact + the traced nib ties 16 with one free end each); it must not. Tested in `test-pdf-poche.ts` section 10b (4 of 16 with 0 problems against 16 of 16 with one free end).
- **A conflict removes the traced opening too** (`snap.ts` `conflictAt`): a traced opening lying on an opening walls.ts reported as a conflict (within 3 px plus half the wall's thickness across, overlapping it by more than 3 px) is removed, in the merge and in the whole-plan fallback, and listed on that conflict (`Coverage.conflicts[i].traced`, present only when non-empty). `conflictText` counts it: "Window 1 (1.20 m) conflicts with a wall ending inside it; not read. 1 traced opening at the same place was removed too." The review's `li` carries `data-traced`.
- **Level notes are never room names** (`analysePdf.ts` `withoutLevel`, used by `pdfLabels`, so PDF text only: image and OCR input are unchanged). A text line that is a level note (starts with FFL, SSL, FL, LEVEL or LVL, dotted or not, or is a bare signed number such as "+0.000") is no label; one at the END of a name's own line ("Bathroom FL -0.020", "Living +0.150") is stripped. A room really named "Level …" would lose its name. Fixture: plan-levels (plan-filled with five level notes beside the names); test-pdf-poche section 11.

## The plan verdict (step H.1)
- **"Matches lines in the PDF" is not "usable".** A real run said "52 of 52 walls read exactly from the PDF (100%)" while the plan had 38 free wall ends, 17 detached groups, 23 gaps not read, 12 windows rejected and one "room" made of disconnected blocks. The review now judges the BUILT plan first: `planVerdict(plan, coverage)` in `src/lib/pdf/verdict.ts` (pure) returns `{ level: "good" | "check" | "unusable", reasons, facts }`, and `verdictText` writes it ("Not usable: the walls form 6 separate pieces (the largest holds 17% of the wall length), 12 free wall ends and no closed room.").
- **Inputs:** closed rooms (`deriveRooms`), free wall ends (`freeEnds`, moved here from snap.ts: an end shared with no other wall within 1 cm), pieces (walls grouped by shared ends) and the share of wall length in the largest, kept detached groups (`Coverage.detached` with `dropped: false`; a group left out on title-block evidence is not a reason), and rejected openings = `Coverage.conflicts` + `Coverage.refused` (new in H.1: PDF openings `openingProblem` or a missing wall turned away). Gaps not read are not counted on their own: a wide one leaves free ends, a narrow one closes as wall.
- **Fixed thresholds** (`RULES`, reasons in the code, tuned to no file): **Good** = one connected building, at most 2 free ends (`maxFreeEndsGood`; a nib or wing wall is a real detail), at least one room, no rejected opening and no kept detached group. **Not usable** = the largest group under 70% of the wall length (`minLargestShare`), or zero rooms, or more than 10 free ends (`maxFreeEndsUsable`). **Check** = anything else (several pieces with at least 70% in the largest, 3 to 10 free ends, a rejected opening, a kept detached group).
- **Review screen** (`PdfPanels.tsx` `VerdictBanner`, `data-testid="plan-verdict"`, `data-level`): first in the side panel, in plain words with its reasons. The walls line is `coverageLine`: "N walls match lines in the PDF (X% of wall length); …", never "exactly", and whenever the verdict is not Good it carries `coverageCaveat` in the same paragraph (`coverage-line-text` is the sentence, `coverage-line-caveat` the caveat; `data-verdict` on the paragraph). Since step S.1 the openings line says "N openings match features in the PDF" and carries `openingsCaveat` the same way (`coverage-openings-text`, `coverage-openings-caveat`).
- **Not usable asks twice.** Build's button reads "Build anyway"; the first click only shows the verdict again (`build-anyway-note`) and turns the button into "Confirm: build anyway"; the second builds exactly as "Build model" does. A new scale or reading disarms it (`armedFor` is the review object). Good and Check keep "Build model" and one click.
- **Image input and scanned PDFs have no `Coverage`, so no verdict** and are unchanged. The baseline `tests/fixtures/pdf/test-plans-baseline.json` also records each file's `verdict`; `e2e-pdf-import` fails when it gets worse (its numbers are unchanged).
- **Tests:** `scripts/test-pdf-verdict.ts` (in `npm test`): every rule at its boundary on the sample plan plus added walls, `plan-strips.pdf` (six 3 m strips touching nothing; Not usable, in the review's build and in the PDF's-walls-alone build where all 6 are "exact" and the old line read "6 of 6 walls read exactly (100%)"), the wording, and the verdict recorded for every fixture (32 Good, 37 since step S.1's five new fixtures, 3 Check: plan-outbuilding, plan-stem-in-window, plan-chatgpt-poche; 1 Not usable: plan-strips; 2 with nothing to judge in Node: plan-outlined needs OCR, plan-scanned is a picture). `plan-strips.pdf` is written by `make-pdf-fixtures.ts` (option `strips`) but is NOT in `truth.json`, because the generic "every fixture reads its walls exactly" loops do not apply to a fixture with no building. `e2e-pdf-import` checks every fixture's level and the Build anyway flow at 1440 and 390.
- **Limits:** the verdict knows nothing about openings' correctness, only that some were left out; it counts pieces by shared ends only (a wall ending on another's middle without a split counts as free); the thresholds are general rules of thumb that no real CAD PDF has tested.

## Walls drawn as one stroke (step S.1)
- **The convention** (`walls.ts`, header "Step S.1"): a straight, axis-aligned, solid (not dashed), stroked (not filled) segment whose width at the drawing's scale is 50–600 mm (`WALL_RULES.stroke`) is a wall's CENTRE LINE, as thick as the stroke is wide: a piece as it stands, like a filled rectangle (`band`, also `rect`). A thinner stroke stays annotation or furniture; a band is never a face as well. The cap is ignored: butt caps leave a half-thickness notch at a corner and round or square caps paint past the end, but the geometry is the centre line, and rule 7 joins ends to the centre lines' crossing (L, T to the centre line or to the face, X). Gaps in a stroke are openings by rule 4, classified as before (line-leaf doors with hinge and side, three-line windows). A band's two edges are lines for rule 3, like a filled rectangle's.
- **Heavy pens are faces, not walls.** A thick stroke with a partner of the same width (within 1 mm) parallel to it, at least twice its width and at most 0.6 m away, overlapping by 60% of the shorter, is one face of a wall drawn in pairs with that pen (a 0.5 mm pen is 50 mm at 1:100): it pairs as before. A band lying wholly inside a paired wall's footprint (the end of a heavy-pen outline) is dropped.
- **Rule 7c, treads** (`furnitureOf`, after the islands): two pairs of one axis with the same span and thickness whose bodies overlap across are not walls (a wall's body is solid); that is what a run of evenly spaced thin lines pairs into: stair treads 0.22 m apart make overlapping 0.44 m slabs. A free-standing run was already an island; one standing on a wall, joined to it, is dropped here, reported in `furniture` as "treads" ("Stair treads or hatching" on the review screen) and the joints are made again without it, so the wall is whole.
- **Fixtures (all SYNTHETIC, `make-pdf-fixtures.ts` option `stroke`, `stairs`):** `plan-single-stroke.pdf` (230 / 115 mm strokes at 1:100, butt caps, line-leaf doors in all four hinge / side combinations, three-line windows, twelve treads standing on the south wall, the furniture set, ticked mm dimensions), `-round` and `-square` (the same caps aside), `-mixed` (exterior walls as paired outlines drawn with a 1.5 pt pen, 53 mm at 1:100; interior single strokes). Each reads 14 of 14 walls within 0.5 mm and 0.5 mm in thickness, 9 of 9 openings with hinge and side, four rooms to 1e-6 m², verdict Good (`scripts/test-pdf-stroke.ts`, in `npm test`, failing first: 0 of 14 walls, 27 free ends, no room, Not usable). `plan-traced-wall.pdf` (option `pictureWalls`) draws w-KM as an embedded picture, the wall only the tracing can see; it took over every traced-wall check plan-single-line had, whose one 0.1 m stroke is now read from the PDF (14 of 14 exact).
- **Frozen snapshot.** `scripts/test-pdf-snapshot.ts` (in `npm test`) compares every fixture's VectorWalls and build (walls, openings, coverage counts and notes, rooms, verdict) with `tests/fixtures/pdf/reading-snapshot.json`. A fixture whose reading should change goes in its `CHANGED` list with why (today only plan-single-line); fixtures not in the snapshot are listed. Re-freeze with `SNAPSHOT_WRITE=1` only after a reviewed change.
- **Limits:** diagonal strokes are not read as walls; two parallel single-stroke walls of one width, overlapping and at least twice that width but under 0.6 m apart, read as one wall's heavy-pen faces; a heavy-pen wall thinner than twice its pen is read as two thin walls; treads more than 0.3 m apart pair into adjacent slabs that don't overlap and are not caught. No real CAD PDF has been read, and the first holdout file is used up (this step was written after it failed; it can no longer serve as an unseen test).

## Arrowheads, two readings, T breaks and flush jambs (step Q.4a)
- **The real file that exposed these is used up, and no fresh holdout has been run.** All four fixes come from one real-file diagnosis (`scripts/q3-report.ts`). Each is a general drawing convention proven failing-first on its own SYNTHETIC fixture (`scripts/test-pdf-q4.ts`, in `npm test`). That real file can no longer serve as an unseen test.
- **1. Arrow tips** (`scale.ts` `arrowEnds`). A dimension is measured, in this order, between:
  1. Filled arrowheads' tips. An arrowhead is a filled path with exactly three corners at each end of the line. Its tip and the middle of its base lie on the line's axis (within 0.1 text heights). The tip or the base is within 0.5 text heights of the line's end, and it points outward.
  2. Ticks or extension lines.
  3. The line's own length.

  `DimensionMatch.measure` is `"arrows" | "ticks" | "line"`, and the table says "between arrow tips". Arrow size is free, held only to 0.25–6 text heights: plan-arrows draws 2.5 h and 3.5 h, and plan-chatgpt-poche's are 0.9 h. A line that stops at the arrowheads' bases was short by both of them. A wide enough arrowhead's base also used to pass as a tick.
  - Fixture plan-arrows: the imperial house with only two dimensions ("10668 mm", 28'-0"), text in a gap in the line, extension lines shorter than the text height and set back. It was 1185 and 1660 mm short, with the scale "good" but 18% off.
  - plan-chatgpt-poche's rows with arrowheads (10 of 12) now say "between arrow tips". Their lengths are unchanged, because its tips sit at the line ends.
- **2. Few readings** (`scale.ts` `combineScale`, `FEW`). With fewer than 3 readings, "good" also needs them within 1% of each other (largest over smallest). Otherwise the scale is "check", with "Two readings disagree by X%: the drawing may not be to scale, or a dimension spans something else. Confirm the scale." The value is still their median. This applies to PDF dimensions only; the image pipeline's bands are unchanged.
  - Fixture plan-dims-disagree: two dimensions, one drawn 3% long and one 3% short (6.2% apart).
  - It is deliberately NOT in `truth.json`: its median is 0.09% off, so the "walls within 0.5 mm" loops don't apply.
  - No existing fixture changed status.
- **3. T breaks** (`walls.ts` `mergeLines` `tBreak`). A break under 0.45 m still closes as wall. It is left out of `gaps` when a perpendicular piece stands in it that:
  - is as thick as the break (within 1 mm),
  - has its centre inside the break,
  - and ends at this wall (within half its thickness plus `WALL_RULES.meet`).

  CAD draws a room's outline as lines, so a face breaks for every wall meeting it at a T. Breaks of any other width, and breaks with no wall meeting them, are still reported.
  - Fixture plan-t-breaks: walls drawn as face lines broken at all six Ts, plus a real 300 mm gap and a 100 mm gap away from any T.
  - plan-chatgpt-flat's one reported gap (115 mm) was exactly such a T break, so it is gone; `test-pdf-snapshot.ts` lists the change.
- **4. Flush jambs** (`snap.ts` `place()`, `ROUND_SLACK`). Offsets and widths are rounded to 0.1 mm, so a jamb flush with a T stem's face could land up to 0.075 mm outside the usable span, and the opening was refused. A jamb outside by at most 0.1 mm is now put on the span's end. Every other opening keeps its rounded offset and width exactly.
  - Fixture plan-flush-jamb: the imperial house (106.68 mm partitions, off the 0.1 mm grid) with A–C as one wall and a window flush with w-BI's face. It was refused 0.04 mm out, and the verdict was Check.
- **Limits:**
  - An arrowhead must be a filled closed triangle. Open (stroked) arrows, dots and 45° obliques are not arrowheads, and obliques are not ticks either (ticks are perpendicular), so such a line falls back to its extension lines or its own length.
  - The 1% rule needs 3 readings to step aside: two that are both wrong by the same amount still agree.
  - A T break is recognised only at a perpendicular wall; a diagonal wall's break is still reported.
  - The flush-jamb slack does not reach a jamb drawn more than 0.1 mm into the T.
  - Synthetic fixtures only.

## The review off the main thread (step W.1)
- **Nothing of a vector PDF's review runs on the page's thread.** `src/lib/pdf/review.ts` `reviewPdf({ analysis, pdf, scale, carry? })` is, unchanged, what the import page used to compute itself: `buildPdfPlan`, `planVerdict` and `modelRows` WITHOUT overrides, and with `carry` (Build model only, the first table's overrides) the printed dimensions carried as takeoffs (the page's old `withPdfTakeoffs`). ## Project files: Save project and Open project (step E.1)

## Known limitations
- The hollow fill cannot tell a wall whose gap is outside the accepted range from furniture drawn as long parallel lines. Both are just two long lines with white between them.
- Walls whose gap is more than 3x the commonest gap are missed. On a plan with lots of narrow line pairs (window lines, shelving), the commonest gap can be narrower than the real walls, and then real walls are skipped.
- Room-size labels give no scale for open-plan rooms, rooms bounded mostly by windows, or rooms whose edges are single thin lines. The label is measured against the walls around it, and those rooms don't have enough wall to measure against.
- OCR sometimes splits a size label into fragments, and then the size is not read.
- When no scale can be worked out, the fallback is the user clicking a wall and typing its length.
- `OpeningCandidate.widthPx` is the clear gap between the wall ends as drawn, in deskewed pixels. A wall end next to a T-joint can be snapped into the joint, which makes that opening up to about one wall thickness too wide.
- Gap pairing has no maximum width, so two collinear free ends across a room can pair into a fake door (06 has a 481 px one). `buildPlan` (`src/lib/blueprint/toPlan.ts`) classifies each gap by real width once the scale is known: up to 2.4 m is a normal opening; over 2.4 m up to 4 m is a wide opening, kept and listed in `report.wideOpenings` (a wide passage stays a door, because the Plan has only door and window kinds); over 4 m is not bridged, its two wall ends stay free, and it is listed in `report.droppedPairs`.
- A body drag moves the whole **wall run**: `wallRun` (`src/lib/plan/edit.ts`) finds every piece one straight wall was split into at its T-junctions (collinear within 1°, sharing a joint within 1 cm; a stem at the joint neither joins the run nor breaks it), and `dragWallBody` slides all of them by the same perpendicular offset, so no piece tilts. Dragging a joint **handle** still moves only the walls joined at that joint, so a collinear neighbour tilts — that is what moving a vertex means, and it is expected.
- A joint shared by more than two non-parallel walls can only keep one of them pointing the same way during a body drag. `dragWallBody` keeps the least parallel one (its line crosses at the steepest angle) and the rest follow the joint, so their directions change.
- A wall body drag checks the 0.2 m minimum at 64 points along the slide, so a joining wall that dips below the minimum and comes back inside one 64th of a drag step is not caught.
- Drawing never splits the NEW wall. A wall that would cross an existing wall is refused (tested); so, by the same check, is one passing through an existing joint or touching an existing free end with its middle (not separately tested). Draw up to the meeting point and carry on from there.
- Drawing checks centre lines only: a wall drawn parallel to an existing one and closer than their thicknesses is allowed, and the two overlap visually.
- A T-junction on a diagonal wall keeps the landing exactly on the wall's line, so its coordinates are not on the 1 cm grid.
- Flip changes only the side a door opens to. Which jamb it hangs on is `Opening.hinge`, changed by the panel's own Hinge side control (step M.1d); before that it was always the jamb nearer the wall's `a`.
- In 3D a door leaf stands only about 17° open, so at the default camera Flip is a small change on screen; the e2e checks the leaf's real rotation in the scene graph instead.
- A wall edit keeps each opening inside its wall (`clampOpening`), but not inside its usable span, and it can push two openings on a shortened wall into overlap. That is reported as a warning (the validator's overlap check), not prevented. Not covered by an e2e.
- Openings imported by `buildPlan` can be up to about one wall thickness too wide and can reach into a corner past the usable span. They are kept as imported; the first move or resize clamps them into it.
- Openings are placed only in the 2D plan. In 3D they are selected (Select tool), resized face by face (Push/Pull) and slid along their wall (Move), since step 4.7b.
- At low zoom (the phone with its sheet open, about 15 px/m) the 20 px touch end-handle reach covers about 1.3 m, so a tap on a short stretch of wall near a corner grabs the corner's handle rather than the wall body. This is 4.3 behaviour, unchanged; zoom in to pick that stretch.
- The swing of a door near a corner can reach over the joining wall. A press physically on that wall still picks the wall; a press just beside it, inside the swing, picks the door.
- The arrow keys nudge a selected wall but not a selected opening; drag it or type its position.
- Door versus window is a guess from the pixels: a double door drawn in thin lines can read as a window, and grey outlines on wall faces can read as doors. The review screen lets the user change the kind.
- Walkthrough: windows are solid at every height; a sill-0 window you could step through in reality still blocks.
- Walkthrough: no stairs or levels; the plan is one floor and the camera stays on it.
- Walkthrough: furniture (`plan.items`) is not collision yet.
- Walkthrough: doors narrower than 0.5 m are solid (listed in `narrowDoors`).
- Walkthrough: a body that reaches a door jamb's end within one substep (≤ 0.1 m) is nudged sideways into the doorway rather than stopped, because the jamb's end is its shortest exit. It never stands in the doorway further off centre than the clear half-width (`width / 2 − radius`), but at that offset it can still get through after the nudge (the sample's front door does).
- Walkthrough: doors do not open or close. The leaf is drawn standing a little open and is never collision, so you walk through it.
- Walkthrough: under about 20 fps every frame is capped at 0.05 s, so walking runs slower than 1.4 m/s in real time rather than jumping.
- Walkthrough: a wall end is extended by half its thickness even where a collinear neighbour has a door flush at that joint, which narrows that door by the same amount.

The project's type scale is large (`text-3xl` = 54 px), so the headline is `text-2xl sm:text-4xl` and the sheet keeps 3.6 m of paper above the plan for it.

## Landing polish (step L.2)
- **Every frame is fitted so nothing is cropped** (`cameraAt(b, plan, aspect, reserve)`, `src/lib/landing/blueprint.ts`). The direction still eases from straight down to 56° tilt and 32° turn with one smoothstep, but the distance and the sideways shift are solved at each b by `fitView` (a bisection on the distance; the shift is the middle of the feasible range): the four sheet corners and the model's box (the footprint from the floor to `wallRise(b) × tallest wall`) must be inside the view, the sheet within `SHEET_FIT` (0.96) of it, and, as b passes `CLEAR_BY` (0.25), the model below the headline's band (`reserve` = the share of the stage's height the headline block covers plus 12 px, measured by BlueprintCanvas from the stage's `[data-headline]` element on each frame it renders). The camera never comes nearer than 1.1 × the start's distance at the end. The first frame is still the DOM sheet: the sheet's SVG `viewBox` is the sheet box grown by 1 / `SHEET_FIT` about its centre (a CSS `scale()` did the same but delayed first paint by about 130 ms at 1440, so do not use one on that SVG), `data-sheet-box` holds the real box, and the canvas texture is cut to it. The static end state's `viewBox` holds the sheet as well as the model.
- **Tests.** `test-landing-scene.ts` (in `npm test`) projects the sheet corners and model corners with its own look-at camera at 41 values of b at 1440×900, 1280×720 and 390×844: inside the view, and under the headline band from b = 0.25. `e2e-landing.ts` (E2E_ONLY=fit) does it by pixels at nine positions of the build: the outer ring of the screenshot is the ground colour, no near-black pixel anywhere (an unpainted texture edge), and with the headline text hidden no model-coloured pixel in the headline's band.
- **One headline.** The blueprint scene's `h1` "From one drawing, a whole house" is the page's only h1 and the only place the sentence appears; the mansion's heading is "Every wall starts as a line on a plan" (`landingScenes.ts`). `e2e-landing` checks both.
- **Preloader** (`BlueprintScene.tsx`, `.preloader` in `globals.css`). The wordmark and a thin gilt bar, `fixed inset-0`, `pointer-events-none`, so it moves nothing (layout shift 0.0000, page height equal before and after) and never takes a click. It shows while the 3D canvas is getting ready (the hero is the sheet, server-rendered, so "ready" is the canvas's first frame, `data-ready`) and goes when the canvas is ready, or `PRELOADER_MAX_MS` (2800, `timeline.ts`) after navigation started, or at once for the static mode or a failed canvas; a CSS guard (`animation … var(--preloader-max)`, visibility only) hides it by the same time even if no script ever runs. `motion-reduce:hidden` hides it before any script under reduced motion. No spinner: the bar is time-based, not a byte count. Its animations are on `width` and `visibility`, not `transform` or `opacity`: a compositor animation on a full-screen layer made first paint wait about 170 ms. `data-preloader` on the scene says `showing` or `done:<ms>`.
- **A canvas that fails to load or start** (a blocked chunk, no WebGL after all) is caught by `CanvasBoundary`; the scene falls back to the static mode and the page carries on. `e2e-landing` (E2E_ONLY=failed) blocks every script that holds `WebGLRenderer`.
- **Photographs** (`LandingTour.tsx`). All five have `width`/`height` (2560×1440). Only the first has a real `src` (eager, `fetchPriority="high"`). The others hold a 1 px transparent GIF data URL until `allowed` grows: the lobby when the tour section enters the viewport (IntersectionObserver) or Step inside is pressed, then one room ahead of the scroll (`allow(2 + ceil(progress × steps))`). `loading="lazy"` alone does not work here: the tour starts exactly one screen below the fold, inside Chrome's lazy-load distance, so all five (4.5 MB) were fetched before the first scroll. Before first scroll: 1 photo, 1010 kB (was 5, 4590 kB). The files themselves are unchanged (about 1 MB each; AVIF or smaller JPEGs would cut the bytes further and were not asked for).
- **The tour on a phone** (`gsap.matchMedia`): from 640 px the arch clip-paths and photo push-in are unchanged; under 640 px it is an opacity crossfade, 100% of the screen height of scroll per room, a covered layer set to `visibility: hidden` (so two are painted at most), no clip-path, no photo scaling, and the layers carry `will-change: opacity` only. That last part matters: without it every fade repainted a 3× full-screen photo on the main thread (5 to 11 frames over 50 ms through the tour at 390; with it 0 or 1).
- **Tests and tools.** `e2e-landing.ts` parts: `E2E_ONLY=fit,scene,load,failed,tour` (default all). `load` checks the preloader (seen, gone by 3 s, within a second of the canvas being ready, no layout shift, same page height), the photo requests with real scrolls (only the first at rest and a little way into the build, the lobby once the tour is near, the living room only once the scroll reaches the lobby, all five by the end), and no preloader under reduced motion. `tour` checks the caption does not overlap Step inside, is inside the screen, 13 px, and 4.5:1 or better against the picture (measured on the screenshot: 7.7:1 at 1440, 6.8:1 at 390), the crossfade's layers, and prints frame times. `scripts/landing-report.ts` (needs a production server: `npx next start -p 3100`, `E2E_URL=http://localhost:3100`) prints the images and their bytes before first scroll, JS on the wire, FCP, LCP (and its element) and CLS at 1440 and 390; it opens a throwaway page first, because the browser's own start-up otherwise lands on the 1440 run. Headless Chromium on SwiftShader on a loaded Mac: not a phone's numbers.
- **Limits:** the preloader's end is "the canvas has drawn", not a count of bytes; the first photo's high priority competes with the hero's JavaScript for bandwidth; frame times are headless and noisy (a desktop tour run varies from 15 to 70 frames over 50 ms with the machine's load); the mansion's heading wraps to three lines at 390; at 390 the finished model is about 75% of the width with empty ground below it, because the whole sheet must also fit; not tested on a real phone, Safari or Firefox.

## Studio polish (step N.1)
- **New plan** is Import's first item. `ProjectDialog` mode `new` always asks "Start from the sample plan" or "Empty plan" and warns when `isDirty()`. `projectStore.newPlan(kind)` = one `replacePlan` (one undo step, nothing marked Edited), the new plan becomes the saved baseline, `epoch` goes up so both panes refit. `src/data/emptyPlan.ts` is the empty plan. Before N.1 an old autosave could not be left except by clearing site data.
- **First-run hint** (`StudioHints.tsx`): shown while `showHint(plan, dismissed)` (`src/lib/studioHint.ts`): the untouched sample (`planSignature`, key-order independent, survives an autosave round trip) or an empty plan, until "Got it". Dismissal is `atrium.studioHint` in localStorage (`hintStore.ts`), a view setting, never in the plan. It is an overlay (click-through except its button, hidden while walking), so it moves nothing.
- **Empty plan:** `EmptyPlanCard` shows with no walls and the Select tool. `Scene3D.planBounds` must handle zero walls (Math.min of nothing is Infinity; NaN bounds froze the tab). Anything that takes min/max over walls must guard the empty plan.
- **OCR name noise:** `cleanRoomLabels` (`nameRooms.ts`) runs in `buildFromAnalysis` only (images and scanned pages). It drops labels under `MIN_LABEL_CONFIDENCE` (55, shared with `ocr.ts`), labels with more symbols and digits than letters, and labels of 3 letters or fewer with no known short name (`KNOWN_SHORT`); it strips symbol-only words at a label's ends but keeps a "/" between words. `nameRooms` and the vector-PDF text path (`snap.ts`) do not clean. Tests: `test-name-noise.ts`, `test-new-plan.ts` (in `npm test`), `e2e-newplan.ts` (dev server).
- **Limits:** a real 3-letter room name outside `KNOWN_SHORT` is dropped; Escape from the Wall tool back to Select needs 2D canvas focus; not tested on a real phone, Safari or Firefox.

## Exports: schedule, report, GLB and OBJ (step X.2)
- **Every export is a derived view of the Plan**, made in the browser and saved with the browser's own download (`saveFile` in `src/components/studio/ExportDialog.tsx`); nothing is uploaded or fetched. The Export menu holds exactly: Save project…, Area schedule (CSV), Report (PDF)…, 3D model (GLB)…, 3D model (OBJ)… (`e2e-studio` and `e2e-export` assert the list).
- **The area schedule** (`src/lib/export/schedule.ts`, pure): rooms (name, net area, perimeter) are `deriveRooms(plan)`, the very source of `useDerivedRooms`, the Summary and the room list, so the numbers cannot disagree (`test-exports.ts` asserts exact equality for the sample and plan-filled.pdf). Openings are grouped by kind, width, height and sill to 0.1 mm, with a count. Totals are sums of exact values, rounded once at display. The CSV is two sections (Rooms, a blank line, Openings), values in the units setting with the units in each header, through rows.ts's RFC 4180 `toCsv`; file `<plan>-schedule.csv`. The panel is `SchedulePanel.tsx`, a collapsible section at the bottom of the right panel, closed at first.
- **The report** (`src/lib/export/report.ts`) is written with **pdf-lib** (now a runtime dependency, MIT; it was already a devDependency for the fixtures), A4 landscape, from plan data, never a screenshot. Page 1: title, plan name, date, units; the verdict (`planVerdict` on the plan as it is now, with empty coverage, as Report a problem judges it) and its reasons; the scale source (`scaleSource`: what the plan records, background kind or sample, plus `rescaledText`); counts; and a vector drawing (the landing page's pure `sheetShapes`) with room names and areas. Then the room schedule, the openings schedule, the studio's takeoffs and the PDF dimension rows: measured, reference, difference, status and the override BESIDE the measured value, never in its place. `REPORT_FOOTER` and "Page n of N" on every page. Fonts: Helvetica and Helvetica-Bold, PDF standard fonts, so nothing is embedded or fetched; they encode only Windows-1252, so `clean` turns "−" into "-" and anything else they lack into "?". The date is passed in (the dialog formats it in en-GB), so the test has no time-zone dependence.
- **GLB and OBJ** (`src/lib/export/model3d.ts`): `exportScene` builds each wall with `buildWallMeshData` (the studio's own mesh, openings cut; its `userData.faces` is dropped) named `wall:<id>`, and each room floor with `buildFloorGeometry` named `floor:<room id>` with `{ roomId, name, netArea }` in userData (glTF extras), at y = 0, in metres, Y up, world (plan x, height, plan y). GLB through three's `GLTFExporter` (binary), OBJ through `OBJExporter` (one `o` per mesh, no .mtl). Not included, and the dialog says so: imported models, door and window frames, leaves and glass, ceilings. three's GLTFLoader strips ":" and "." from names when it LOADS a file, so tests read the names from the GLB's JSON chunk.
- **Lazy loading.** `ExportDialog` imports `report.ts` and `model3d.ts` with `import()` only when Download is pressed: pdf-lib is its own on-demand chunk (about 174 kB gzip), the two exporters another (about 12 kB). The schedule, its panel and the dialog shell are in `/studio`'s first load.
- **Float32.** glTF stores positions as 32-bit floats, so a floor area summed from the GLB's triangles is only good to about 1e-7 relative (the sample: 5.9e-6 m² off 74.695 m²). Exact areas travel as extras (JSON doubles); `test-exports.ts` checks those to 1e-6 m² and the triangles to 1e-6 relative.
- **Tests:** `scripts/test-exports.ts` (in `npm test`, written failing first; it gives Node a small FileReader stand-in, which GLTFExporter needs to write a GLB) and `scripts/e2e-export.ts` (dev server; real downloads at 1440 and 390, PDF text through pdf.js, GLB through GLTFLoader in Node; screenshots `export-menu-<w>.png`, `export-schedule-<w>.png`, `export-dialog-<w>.png` in /tmp/studio).
- **Limits:** the report's verdict judges the plan alone (the import's coverage, including detached groups and rejected openings, is not kept in the plan); the scale source is what the plan records, not which dimension the import used; the drawing is not to a printed scale and room labels can overlap in small rooms; characters outside Windows-1252 print as "?"; door types (double, sliding) are not in the plan, so the schedule says Door or Window; no DXF; GLB/OBJ carry no openings' frames, no textures and no imported models; not tested on a real phone, Safari or Firefox.
